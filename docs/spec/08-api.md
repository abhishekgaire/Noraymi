## API

One JSON API over HTTPS serves every screen. Every write is safe to retry, every change is audited, and live events tell screens what to fetch again.

**Conventions**

- **Paths** are scoped by venue: `/v1/venues/{venueId}/…`. The API checks the caller's membership, then sets the venue for the row-level policy. Guest routes live under `/v1/public/…` and take a venue slug, a signed token or a room-session cookie.
- **Principals.** Every route declares which principals may call it ([Tenancy and access](02-tenancy-access.md)), and any other caller gets `403`. Staff routes also check the caller's role (owner, manager, bartender, front_desk or staff) against `role_permissions`.
- **Sign-in.** Owners and managers use a session cookie on the web and a bearer token in the desktop app. There are no passwords: the email names the account, and the proof is a passkey (user verification required) or an authenticator-app code sent together with a one-time code emailed first. Every session records how it was opened (`passkey`, `authenticator`, `pin` or `badge`), lasts 12 hours and locks after 30 idle minutes (`401 session_locked`; `401 session_expired` once it's over). Staff get a short session from the device's signature plus a badge tap or their name and PIN, ended when the device locks or someone else's badge takes over. A PIN session never reaches settings, team changes or exports, even for a manager; those, and deciding approvals, need the passkey session (`403 forbidden`, "Admin needs a passkey"; an authenticator session is refused the same way). Large refunds, exports, team changes and card-fee changes ask for the passkey again: the write carries an `X-Step-Up` token from `POST /v1/auth/step-up`, good once and for five minutes, or answers `403 step_up_required`. Guests use a token scoped to one booking or one room session. An owner who loses every sign-in method recovers with one of ten single-use recovery codes (`POST /v1/auth/recover`, no session) or through a second owner of the same venue (`POST /v1/auth/recover/second-owner`, in a passkey session with a step-up); either way the recovery is ready 48 hours later, every owner and manager of the owner's venues is emailed at once, and any of those owners can cancel it before then (`POST /v1/auth/recover/cancel`). Once ready, the owner enrols a new passkey or authenticator through `POST /v1/auth/enroll` as if the account were new, which revokes every earlier sign-in method and session. Recovery codes are issued at an owner's first enrollment and after a recovery, shown once, and replaced on demand (`POST /v1/auth/recovery-codes`).
- **Approvals.** A write that needs approval (a comp or void over the reason-only limit, a refund, a clock pause, a tip over the review limit, a paid-out over the limit, a lower party size after gratuity, a card-on-file charge without the guest, an order over a declined hold) doesn't fail. It answers `202 approval_pending` with the approval and who it's waiting for ("Waiting for Andy"), the screen keeps working, and the change is made when the approver approves it on their own phone. `approval.decided` tells the requester's screen.
- **Training mode.** A caller in training mode, by person or by device, gets practice checks numbered T-0012 and so on. Their card payments go to a simulated reader on Stripe's sandbox, never live Stripe, and nothing they do reaches totals, tips or exports. In training, `GET /readers` lists only the simulated readers, a payment naming a live reader is `404 not_found`, and `POST /readers/{r}/test-card` (`{ declined? }`, training only) presents Stripe's test card to a simulated reader.
- **Idempotency.** Every POST and PATCH takes an `Idempotency-Key`, required on money routes. The server stores the key before doing the work, with the venue, the caller and a hash of the request. A duplicate that arrives while the first is running gets `409 in_progress`, the same key with a different body gets `422 key_reused`, and a finished request replays its answer for 7 days.
- **Money and time.** Amounts in integer cents with `currency: "usd"`; times in ISO 8601 with an offset. Every response, and every WebSocket hello, carries `server_time`.
- **Conflicts.** Checks carry `version`; edits send `If-Match` and get `409` if someone else changed the check first.
- **Clients.** Every response carries `min_client_version`. An older staff or desktop app keeps working and updates between business days.
- **Errors.** `{ "error": { "code": "hold_declined", "message": "…", "retryable": false } }`. The codes screens branch on: `module_off` (404), `forbidden` (403), `version_conflict` (409), `in_progress` (409), `alcohol_closed` (409), `cut_off` (409), `ordering_closed` (409, the check is presented), `orders_open` (409, an order is still ringing or held), `checklist_open` (409, a clock-out with its checklist not clear; `details.items` lists what's left), `night_open` (409, a close with a check before closing still open; `details.checks` names each), `room_not_free` (409, the room doesn't fit or isn't free for the time needed), `over_amount_due` (422), `over_refundable` (422, more than the payment captured minus earlier refunds), `key_reused` (422), `approval_pending` (202), `payment_unknown` (202), `reader_busy` (409), `reader_offline` (503), `session_locked` (401), `session_expired` (401) and `step_up_required` (403).
- **Lists** page with a cursor (`?after=`) and at most 100 items per page, and filter with `?status=` or `?state=` where the table has one. Reports run on a read replica with a 5-second limit.

**Live events.** Every change writes a `venue_events` row in the same transaction. One relay, the leader under an advisory lock, reads committed rows in commit order and stamps `seq`. Every API container then tails `venue_events` by `seq`, woken by NOTIFY on its own connection and polling each second as a backstop, and pushes to its own WebSockets. A screen opens one WebSocket, filtered to what its principal may see, so a room tablet gets only its room's channel. A device's socket carries its signature as query parameters on the upgrade (a browser can't set headers on a WebSocket), signed over the URL without them, with the nonce spent once. Each event is small, `{ "seq": 1042, "type": "order.ringing", "id": "…", "entity_version": 7, "at": "…" }`, and the screen fetches the resource again; events never carry the money math. A screen that reconnects sends the last `seq` it saw and gets everything after it (events are kept 72 hours). A gap means a full refetch, and `entity_version` lets a screen ignore a stale read. Refetches are grouped for 250 ms, reconnects back off with jitter, and deploys drain sockets slowly.
