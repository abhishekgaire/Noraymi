## Money rules

One pricing module on the server calculates every amount, and the screens only display what it returns, so the room screen, the board, the receipt and the Z report can never disagree.

1. **Cents and rounding.** Every amount is an integer number of cents. A percentage is applied to a total and rounded half up to the cent. When an amount is divided (split shares, tax and gratuity across categories, tip shares), the leftover cents go to the largest remainders so the parts add up exactly, and on a tie to the first parts: $32.66 in two is $16.33 + $16.33, and $32.67 is $16.34 + $16.33.
2. **Business date.** Every sale, payment, shift and drawer move belongs to the night it happened in: its local time minus the venue's day cutover (06:00), stamped when the row is written. Day-of-week minimums, time bands and specials use the business date and the minutes from midnight at the start of that date, so 12:30 AM early on a Sunday counts as Saturday at 24:30, minute 1,470. Durations come from elapsed time, never from subtracting clock times, so the two daylight-saving nights bill correctly. Times in settings are wall-clock times in the venue's IANA time zone, resolved for each business date by one library, Temporal, through one pinned polyfill on the server and in the apps until every runtime we support ships it. A time in the repeated hour resolves to its first occurrence and one in the skipped hour to the hour after, Temporal's "compatible" rule. On the fall-back night the booking grid shows both 1 AM hours, labeled EDT and EST, and on the spring-forward night it refuses times that don't exist; receipts, room screens and the Z report show EDT or EST on both nights. The scheduler runs in UTC and works out each job's run time per date, so a 1:30 AM job neither runs twice nor gets skipped.
3. **Room time.** Room time is billed from segments. A party-size change, a time band, a room move or a pause closes one segment and opens the next, on the minute. A segment's hourly rate comes from its band's amounts in the venue's rate mode (per person, base plus extra, or flat by room size). At West 4 it's billable guests × the per-person rate, where billable guests is the larger of the party size and the business date's minimum (3 on weeknights and 4 on Friday and Saturday at West 4). In the VIP room, a party at or above the VIP threshold (20 at West 4) pays the VIP flat rate instead. The session bills `round(Σ hourly_cents × minutes / 60)` over its segments, rounded once. The first-hour minimum applies once per session: a session shorter than an hour is topped up to one hour at its first segment's rate.
   - **Billing step.** Each band also sets a billing step of 1, 15, 30 or 60 minutes and a rounding rule (up, nearest or down). With a step above 1 minute, the session's minutes after the first hour are rounded to the step by the rule of the band the session ends in, and the minutes added or taken off bill at the last segment's rate. Segments copy the step and rule, and every check revision stores them in its billing basis. West 4 bills by the minute, so the step changes nothing there.
   - **Party size.** A change closes the segment and shows the new hourly rate and billable minimum. Lowering it after the gratuity applies needs approval (rule 9).
   - **Pauses and faults.** A pause, including one for a fault such as a dead mic, needs a reason and approval. "Comp 15 min of room time" needs no approval of its own: it's a reason-only comp (rule 7) of 15 minutes at the segment's rate, under the same limits as any other.
   - **Resume.** A session ended by mistake can be resumed within 10 minutes with the same room code and check.
4. **Soft end and closing.** The booked end never stops the clock. When a booking or a waitlist party needs the room, staff and the room screen get wrap-up prompts, and time keeps billing by the minute until close-out. Nothing runs past the night's close: bookings must end by it, offers to stay stop 30 minutes before it, and at the close every room goes to wrap-up.
5. **Alcohol.** One server check decides whether an alcohol line may be created: the alcohol window is open, and neither the tab, the room nor the guest it's for is cut off. Every route that creates one runs it: room orders from guests and the host, staff orders, quick sale, tabs, gift orders (checked against the receiving tab), Repeat round and Same again, packages, and moves. A refusal answers `409 alcohol_closed` or `409 cut_off` and is logged in `alcohol_refusals`, and guest screens hide alcohol. An order's sale time is when the bar accepts it.
   - **The 4 AM stop.** When the window closes (4:00 AM at West 4), every alcohol button greys out on every screen, and a job cancels every alcohol order still ringing or held, with `cancel_reason` `alcohol_closed` and nothing charged. The room's screen says "The bar stopped serving alcohol at 4 AM · your order was cancelled, nothing charged", and the bar screens list it under "Cancelled at 4:00 AM". There is no manual Decline after 4 AM. An order that also has non-alcohol items keeps ringing with just those.
   - **Cut-offs.** Staff can cut off a bar tab (`tabs.cut_off_at`), a whole room (`room_sessions.alcohol_cut_off_at`) or one guest in a room (`room_guests.alcohol_cut_off_at`), each with who, when and why, logged as a refusal. A room cut-off stops room, host, staff and gift orders for that room; a guest cut-off stops that guest's own orders while the rest of the room still orders. Every screen for that tab or room shows "Cut off by Andy at 10:30 PM", its alcohol orders still ringing or held are cancelled (`cut_off`), and singing is still fine.
   - **Moves.** Moving an alcohol line onto a cut-off tab or room is refused, with the reason. Any move onto a tab runs the hold-raise check exactly as a send does (rule 12).
   - **Clear-out check.** At the close plus the drinking-up time (4:30 AM at West 4), a job raises the clear-out check on the board and Night: "Walk every room and the bar · no drinks left out". [Done] records who and when in `clear_out_checks`, and the night can't close before it's done.
6. **Orders, songs and presenting the check.**
   - **Orders.** Items are charged at the price when ordered, copied onto the order. The order's lines join the check when the bar accepts it, which is the sale. Asking the room to wait (`held`) isn't a sale: a held order still needs Accept and can't be marked ready or delivered first. A staff order from the bar POS or a room tab is accepted as it's placed. Ready, on its way and delivered only track the run, and delivering never charges. A guest can cancel only while the order is ringing or held, and the bar declines only with a reason the guest sees; either way nothing is charged. A runner who couldn't serve returns the order with a reason, and the bar resolves it as void · not made, void · made (waste, rule 7) or remake (a new ticket, nothing charged again).
   - **Songs.** A song is charged when staff or an adapter mark it started, never when it's queued, and a skipped song costs nothing: a credit held for it comes back. At West 4, each drink bought earns the singer one song credit ("buy a drink, get a song"), and a song started on a credit posts a $0.00 song line.
   - **Minimum spend.** A minimum can be set per room size tier, day and band in the `prices` setting, or by the big-party rule; it's off at West 4. At check-in the session copies the big-party minimum when that rule applies, and otherwise the one in `prices`. Spend toward it is items and songs after comps, before tax and gratuity, and the tile, DeskRoom and the room page show what's left ("$84 to your minimum"). Presenting the check adds any shortfall as a `min_spend` line.
   - **Presenting the check.** Close-out starts with [Present the check], which finalizes it (Room 9's is #1042) and locks ordering on its session, so guests see "Your bill is ready · ordering is closed". It's refused while any order on the session is ringing or held, and lists them first ("accept or cancel it first"). A manager can reopen the check; the next finalize writes a new revision.
   - **After payment.** Paid in full, the room goes to cleaning, which is blocked while any order on the room is ringing, held or on a check that isn't paid. An order accepted after the check is paid goes onto a new check, which staff settle before the room is released.
7. **Comps and voids.** A comp (the house pays for a drink the guest had) or void (the sale is taken back) is a negative line pointing at the original, with a reason and whether the drink was made. Made is a waste flag for reports, never a stock movement, since phase 1 keeps no stock. Up to the reason-only limit ($25 each and $75 a shift per person at West 4), a reason is enough. The shift total counts every reason-only comp and void the person made during their open shift on every screen (the bar POS, the room phone, DeskRoom and the board), so switching screens never resets it. Over either limit, the line waits for approval from a manager other than the requester, on the approver's own phone ([Tenancy and access](02-tenancy-access.md)), and a manager's own requests go to an owner. The same limits cover room time: "Comp 15 min of room time" is $10.00 in Room 5 (4 guests), where a reason is enough, and $30.00 in Room 9 (12 guests), which is over $25 and waits for approval. Unsent drinks aren't on the check yet, so taking one off is just an edit. Once a check is paid, a correction is a refund.
8. **Tax.** Each line has a tax category (`room_time`, `drink`, `food`, `song`, `damage`, `fee` or `surcharge`), and each category's rate and jurisdiction code come from the rule pack. Tax is calculated once per rate for each check revision, on the net lines after comps, then shared across categories and split shares by largest remainder. Each tax line stores its rate, jurisdiction code, taxable base and rule-pack version. Phase 1 taxes room time, drinks and damage fees at 8.875% and doesn't tax the gratuity; the accountant confirms these, and how card surcharges, kept deposits and no-show charges are taxed.
9. **Gratuity.** The venue's rule decides which checks carry one (`pay.gratuity.auto`: off, rooms, parties or all). At West 4 it's 20% on room checks only, so bar tabs never carry one and tip on the reader instead. The base is room time, items and songs after comps, before tax, and never includes a damage fee. Drinks moved in from a bar tab are items of the room check and carry its gratuity. In parties mode, a bar tab asks for its party size when it opens. Each revision stores its basis: the rule, the largest party size recorded and the percent. Lowering the party size after the gratuity applies, or removing it, needs approval and writes a reversal line. A split shares one gratuity line by largest remainder instead of working it out per share. With a gratuity on the check, the reader skips its tip screen, and receipts print "Gratuity included (20%)" and call any tip line "Additional tip (optional)". The Z report's gratuity is the sum of the gratuity lines on the checks that carry one, never a percent of all sales: at West 4, 20% of room time, room drinks and packages sold to rooms, less room comps and refunds.
10. **Card fee.** A credit card surcharge is one rate for every credit payment, capped at the in-person card cost (2.7%) and the card networks' 3%. It's calculated on the amount going onto that card, before any tip, once the reader reports the card as credit, and it's written with its own tax line when that payment is confirmed. A cash discount writes a `cash_discount` line and a matching tax reduction when the cash is taken. With a surcharge on, everywhere a price appears shows the credit price, as GBL §518 requires, and the guest sees the new total before confirming. A refund returns the same share of the fee and its tax.
11. **Deposits.** A deposit is a payment, never a line. At check-in it's allocated to the check. Until then it's money held for the guest (a customer-deposits liability in the journal), not a sale. A kept deposit or a no-show charge becomes a `fee` check with the next check number and a `forfeit` line. A smaller party gets deposit money back only before the refund cut-off; after it, the deposit already paid stays and is allocated at check-in. That allocation follows the check's lines up to the deposit, like a tab hold's, so the check never shows a negative amount due. If the check closes for less than the deposit, the rest becomes a `forfeit` line on a `fee` check, and the manage page says so before the guest confirms the smaller party. Other money paid ahead works the same way: song credit bought at a song price, and later gift cards (phase 2) and stored value (phase 3), sit in a prepaid-value liability, and `prepaid_ledger` records each issue, redemption, expiry and refund, so a sale is recorded only when the value is spent.
12. **Paying a check.** `payment_allocations` records how much of each payment goes to which check. The amount due is the check's lines minus its captured and in-progress allocations, calculated with the check row locked, and no payment may exceed it, tips aside. A bar tab's hold is the exception: its in-progress allocation follows the tab's lines up to the hold, so a $50 hold on an empty tab is allowed and never shows a negative amount due, and at capture the allocation becomes the amount captured. What a tab still owes, its balance, leaves its own hold out: its lines minus the captured and in-progress allocations of every other payment. Closing the tab, the cut-off capture and any payment that replaces the hold all use that balance, and a replacing payment (cash or a new card) takes over the hold's allocation in the same transaction, before the hold is canceled. Settling a check any other way cancels its open card holds. Lines moved onto a tab raise its hold first, exactly as a send does, and a declined raise leaves the move waiting for approval like a new order. A tab moved into a room closes ("Moved to Room 9"), and its hold is canceled once the room has a payment method (the booking's saved card, the room's own hold, or a card tapped for the room); until then its allocation follows the moved lines onto the room's check, up to the hold.
13. **Splits.** A split lives on the server (`check_splits` and `split_shares` in the [Data model](04-data-model.md)), so a paid share survives leaving the pay screen or switching tabs, and the tab list shows "Partly paid · $16.33 of $32.66". An even split divides the amount left to pay in integer cents, with leftover cents going to the first shares by largest remainder: $32.66 ÷ 2 = $16.33 + $16.33. A split by item gives each person their own items and shares room time and anything nobody claimed evenly, with tax and gratuity shared by largest remainder. Each share has its own way to pay, cash included, and its own state, and each card gets its own card-fee calculation. The next charge is always the rest, and "Stop splitting · charge the rest to …" ends the split with the paid shares kept.
   - **Pay my share** (`pay.payShare`, on at West 4). Once the check is presented, a guest on the room page picks "My items" (a by-item share of what they ordered from their phone) or "An even share (1 of N)", sees their share of tax and gratuity, and pays by Apple Pay, Google Pay or card through the Payment Element. An even share is what was left to pay when the check was presented, divided into N in cents, with N starting at the party size, so a share doesn't change as others pay; no payment goes over what's still due. Each payment is an allocation naming the guest from `room_guests`, shown as "Paid by a guest · Kevin (share 1 of 12) $41.55", and the booker's card still guarantees the rest.
14. **Refunds.** A refund starts from a paid check: the lines, then which payment each amount comes off, then a reason. Each payment's refund is capped at what it captured minus its earlier refunds, so Marcus, with only his $120 deposit captured tonight, can get back at most $120. Every refund needs approval from someone other than the requester, on the approver's own phone. Once approved, it writes reversing lines for what it gives back: the item or room time plus its share of tax, gratuity and card fee. Then it refunds the payments, and each refund that succeeds writes a negative `payment_allocations` row for its payment, so the check's amount due stays at zero. Screens show "Refund pending" until Stripe's `refund.updated` says it succeeded, then "Refunded"; one that fails leaves the amount owed to the guest, to retry or pay back in cash. A cash refund is a drawer move.
15. **Cash drawers.** A venue can have several drawers, each on a receipt printer's kick port. West 4 has two: the bar drawer on the bar receipt printer and the front-desk drawer on the front-desk printer. Cash always goes into the drawer at the screen where it's taken, since each shared screen is paired to one drawer, and each drawer move records who took it ("Logged to Maya · bar drawer"). Cash taken on a staff phone goes into that person's staff bank until they hand it in at a drawer, before they clock out. A venue picks one of two models in Admin → Cash drawers, and a change starts with the next business date. With house drawers (West 4), each drawer has one open session that everyone at its screen rings into, and the manager on duty answers for all of them. When the manager on duty changes, the handover counts each drawer blind, closes its session with the count and opens the next with the same cash, and the incoming manager accepts on their own phone. With a drawer per person, each bartender (or servers too, if the setting says so) opens their own session by counting in the starting bank. Only they can take cash into it; anyone without a drawer hands cash to someone who has one. At a shift change the session is counted then, or its tray is pulled (`pulled`) and counted at close while the next person counts in a fresh bank. Either way every count is blind, a difference over the venue's limit needs a note and, if the setting asks, a second counter, and over or short stays on the session: the night's for a house drawer, the owner's for their own. The night can't close until every session is counted, pulled trays included, and the Z report shows each one with its opening, cash taken, paid-outs, drops, count and over or short.
16. **The Z report and after the night closes.** The Z report splits drink sales into room checks and bar tabs, takes the gratuity only from the checks that carry one (rule 9), and leaves out training checks. Until the night closes, the same report runs as a running X report, and the Z report prints only after the close. A late tip, deposit, no-show charge or refund posts to the current business date, with `adjusts_business_date` pointing at the night it belongs to, and the Z report lists these adjustments. Closed nights never reopen. The nightly journal posts to named accounts: sales by category, sales tax payable by jurisdiction, gratuity payable, tips payable, customer deposits, prepaid value, Stripe clearing, cash, cash over and short, comps and refunds. Each payout gets its own journal, from Stripe clearing to the bank, net of Stripe's fees.
