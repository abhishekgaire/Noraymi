# Gap review

Sep 26, 2026 · Four independent reviews of this spec, merged before any code is written

Four reviewers read the whole spec, the blueprint and every design screen, each from one angle: money and Stripe flows; reliability, offline and operations; security, privacy and New York compliance; and product coverage against the designs. None of them wrote the spec, and each checked this merged list against their own findings. They filed 77 findings and checked outside facts against Stripe, New York State and federal sources on Sep 26, 2026. With duplicates merged, that’s **65 gaps: 8 Blockers, 33 High, 23 Medium and 1 Low.** None of them reopens a decision you’ve made; they’re about the design that carries those decisions out.

**Status, Sep 26:** all 65 gaps are fixed, and they have been checked twice. The four reviewers first checked their own findings against the fixes and found a missing piece in 40 of their 80 findings, plus 22 new problems. Two more reviewers, who hadn't seen the work, then checked that second round: they confirmed 46 of 55 items and found 9 still partly done, 7 new contradictions and 5 wording slips. All of those are fixed too, in the Spec tab (revision 139), the design screens (canvas version 37) and the blueprint (revision 50). Where each gap is fixed and What the re-check found, below, have the details. Still open are the 11 questions under Questions for the lawyer and accountant, which also sit in the spec's Open technical questions and the blueprint's Open decisions, and 6 confirmations only Stripe can give. The question of which readers are in Stripe's P2PE program is closed: Stripe's current P2PE manual lists the S700, S710 and WisePOS E.

The Blockers fall in three places:

- **Money when something fails.** A tap that times out, a cash payment against a card hold and a night close that meets open tabs can each charge a guest twice or lose the charge (B2–B4).
- **Staying up.** The internet-drop plan relies on a reader setting that’s off by default and a router the spec never sets up. Offline mode has three writers, and nothing covers our own cloud going down (B5–B7).
- **Law and access.** Nothing enforces the 4 AM alcohol stop, and staff PINs on shared screens can be guessed in an evening (B1, B8).

Severity follows the review brief. **Blocker:** would lose money, break the law, leak data or stop a live night. **High:** a serious bug, or rework later if it isn’t designed now. **Medium:** specify before its milestone. **Low:** nice to fix.

## What the re-check found

After the first round of fixes, each reviewer checked the 20 gaps they had raised against the revised spec, the screens and the blueprint. None was left unfixed, but half came back only partly fixed, and the check found 22 new problems. The second round fixed all of them.

| Reviewer | Fixed | Partly fixed | New problems |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Money and payments | 13 | 7 | 6 |
| Reliability and offline | 13 | 7 | 6 |
| Security and compliance | 9 | 11 | 6 |
| Product coverage | 5 | 15 | 4 |

The most serious new problems, and what changed:

- **One venue's data could reach another's.** The owner-reports database rule allowed writes from any organization; on a test Postgres 16, the security reviewer copied a guest from one venue into another. The rule now applies only to read-only transactions and to `owner_venues()`.
- **Bar tabs would not open.** Stripe requires `allow_redisplay` when a reader saves a card for later. Tabs now pass `limited`, and the bar screen shows the consent line the reader can't.
- **Offline orders could be charged twice.** An order rung on a phone and also queued on the offline bar computer passed every replay check. Replayed orders now land as `held` until a bartender confirms them.
- **Ten wrong PINs silenced the bar alarm.** The pause now stops only PIN sign-in, and a PIN reset goes to the person's own phone instead of whoever taps the name first.
- **A smaller party could get deposit money back inside the refund cut-off, then not show.** After the cut-off, the deposit stays and comes off the bill at check-in.
- **The payment page shared the website's origin.** It now has its own.
- **Time bands had two definitions.** They now count wall-clock minutes from midnight of the business date.
- **Screens lagged the spec.** Orders joined the tab on delivery, the night close ignored open tabs, the drawer handover showed the expected cash before the count, and the homepage kept a refused promotion. The canvas now matches the spec.

The product reviewer's note that the blueprint was unchanged came from an old local copy. The live blueprint already had the fixes for H29 and H33.

**The second check.** Two reviewers who hadn't seen the work checked the second round against the spec, the screens and the blueprint. What mattered most, and what changed:

- **A tab its hold fully covered showed $0 due.** The cut-off capture would have taken nothing, and cash or a new card would have been refused. A tab's balance now leaves out its own hold, and a replacing payment takes over the hold's allocation.
- **Every report on the replica would have read across venues.** A replica runs every transaction read-only, which was the owner-reports rule's only test. The rule now also needs `app.scope = 'org'`, which only owner-report routes set.
- **Payout matching had no user to scope it.** It ran under the owner's scope but as a job. It now maps each payment to its venue through a definer resolver.
- **A kept deposit could exceed a smaller party's bill.** The deposit now follows the check's lines, and any rest becomes a `forfeit` line the manage page warns about.
- **Screens still missing pieces now have them:** Tips to enter and My tips on the staff phone, the headcount on the board with the venue limit in Admin, all 11 service texts in Admin → Texts, and one big-party deposit rule on both calendars.

## Blockers

### B1 · Nothing enforces the 4 AM alcohol stop

- **Where:** Settings, rule packs (`alcohol.lastSale`, `drinkingUpMin`); Money rules (soft end); API (orders, tabs, quick sale).
- **Gap:** The rule pack stores New York’s 4 AM close and 30-minute drinking-up time, but no route, job or state reads them. A sale is recorded when the bar marks the order delivered, the soft end has no ceiling at close, and Admin has no last-call field, though Setup tells owners they can set an earlier one there.
- **What goes wrong:** At 3:58 AM Room 9 orders 4 Jäger Bombs. The bar accepts at 4:02 and delivers at 4:06, so the check records an alcohol sale at 4:06 AM and the software becomes the evidence of a §106(5) violation. The booking page also sells a 12:30 AM start for up to 6 hours.
- **Fix:** Add `alcohol_window(venue, at)`, built from the rule pack, venue hours, dated overrides and a new `hours.lastCall`, and resolved as wall-clock time in the venue’s zone (never “opening + 14 h”, which is an hour off on DST nights). Every route that creates alcohol lines returns `409 alcohol_closed` outside it, and guest phones and tablets hide alcohol at the cut-off, since §106(5) also bars offering it for sale. Record the sale at `orders.accepted_at`, and auto-cancel alcohol orders nobody accepted by the cut-off. At close every room goes to wrap-up; at close + 30 min the Board shows a clear-out check someone must log. Cap booking ends, availability and waitlist offers at close. Add `alcohol_refusals` and `tabs.cut_off_at`.
- **Found by:** the product and security reviewers, independently. [ABC Law §106](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABC/106)

### B2 · A payment whose result is unknown can be charged twice

- **Where:** Payment flows (room close-out, bar tab, paying with a different card); Stripe setup, step 5 (idempotency keys); API errors `reader_offline` and `reader_busy`.
- **Gap:** Stripe documents three cases where the outcome isn’t known: `terminal_reader_timeout` can be a false negative, a `connection_error` may still have authorized, and a reader that drops mid-payment leaves its action `in_progress` with no webhook. The spec treats all three as plain errors and lets staff try another reader, the card on file or a new PaymentIntent. Keys of the form `pay_<uuid>:<step>` also replay the first attempt after a decline and collide on the second hold increase.
- **What goes wrong:** At 1:30 AM Room 9 taps an Amex for $498.60 as the Wi-Fi drops. The server gets a timeout, staff pick card on file, and both PaymentIntents succeed: the guest pays $997.20 and disputes. Separately, after a Visa declines, retrying with the same key returns Stripe’s saved response, and the reader never asks for the next card.
- **Fix:** Add `payment_attempts(payment_id, check_id, portion_key, attempt_no, action, reader_id, idem_key, amount, state)` with a partial unique index on the check and portion while the state is started or unknown, so each check portion has at most one unfinished attempt, and an unknown attempt keeps its in-progress allocation (B3) until it resolves. Timeouts, connection errors, 5xx responses and a webhook missing after 20 s set `unknown`; the server polls the reader action and the PaymentIntent every 2 s for up to 2 min, and staff see “Status unknown, don’t retry”. Offer another method only after `cancel_action` and the PaymentIntent cancel both succeed. Keys become `<payment_id>:<action>:<attempt_no>`, with increments keyed by target amount. Our `idempotency_keys` table inserts the key first, with a request hash, so a concurrent duplicate gets 409.
- **Found by:** the reliability and money reviewers. [Stripe: server-driven payments](https://docs.stripe.com/terminal/payments/collect-card-payment?terminal-sdk-platform=server-driven), [Stripe: idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests)

### B3 · Payments aren’t tied to checks by amount

- **Where:** Data model (`payments.check_id`, `checks.status`); Money rules (splits); Payment flows (room close-out, bar tab step 6).
- **Gap:** Nothing records how much of each payment applies to which check. A payment in progress doesn’t reserve its amount, settling a check another way doesn’t cancel its card hold, and lines can still land on a paid check.
- **What goes wrong:** A guest pays an $86 tab in cash; the $50 hold stays, and night close captures $86 on the card as well. Two staff tap the last $166.20 share of a split on two readers at once, and both succeed. A drink delivered after Room 9 paid reopens the check with the party gone.
- **Fix:** Add `payment_allocations(payment_id, check_id, amount_cents)`. Balance = lines − captured allocations − in-progress allocations, computed under `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the check; refuse any payment above it (tips aside). Settling a check by any other tender cancels its holds through the outbox. Night close captures only unpaid checks, at `min(balance + tip, hold + overcapture allowance)`. Finalizing locks ordering, and delivering onto a paid check forces a new check or an explicit charge.
- **Found by:** the money reviewer. [Stripe: refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds) (a refund doesn’t return the processing fee)

### B4 · Open bar tabs block night close, and there’s no “waiting for tip” state

- **Where:** Payment flows (bar tab, steps 5–6); API (night close is refused while rooms or tabs are open); the blueprint’s 4 AM auto-close.
- **Gap:** Step 6 says night close captures every open hold, walkouts included, but the API refuses to close while any tab is open, and the blueprint adds a 4 AM auto-close. A walked-out tab can only be closed by a night close that the same tab blocks, and nothing sits between “receipt printed, guest writing a tip” and “captured”.
- **What goes wrong:** Saturday 3:55 AM: 25 tabs are closed on paper receipts and 2 walked out. The bartender types in 10 tips and goes home. The night can’t close, so there’s no Z report, and by Monday the non-Visa holds pass `capture_before` and Stripe cancels them. If close instead captures current totals, every tip not yet typed in is lost to staff.
- **Fix:** Tab states `open → awaiting_tip → captured`, or `capture_failed`. A “Tips to enter” queue with a photo of each signed receipt; a tip over 25% or $50, or one entered more than 2 h late, needs manager approval (H21). A job at the tab cut-off (for example 4:30 AM) captures walkouts, and a sweeper at `capture_before` − 12 h captures anything still waiting, at tip 0, and flags it. Night close needs zero `open` tabs but allows `awaiting_tip`, and a tip entered after the Z posts to the next business date.
- **Found by:** the money reviewer; the sweeper also by reliability. [Stripe: server-driven payments](https://docs.stripe.com/terminal/payments/collect-card-payment?terminal-sdk-platform=server-driven) (uncaptured holds expire after two days)

### B5 · The internet-drop plan’s two fallbacks aren’t set up

- **Where:** Scope and architecture (when the internet drops, steps 1–2); Stripe setup, step 4; Testing and operations (outage drill); Setup, step 8.
- **Gap:** Stripe leaves cellular off on every location by default. It has to be enabled with `cellular[enabled]=true` on the Terminal Configuration, and Stripe bills cellular per reader each month; step 4 only sets tipping. The cellular failover router appears nowhere else: not in setup, the `devices` table, heartbeats, costs or monitoring. Stripe’s docs describe the S710 falling back when Wi-Fi is lost, but not what it does when the Wi-Fi stays up and the internet behind it drops.
- **What goes wrong:** Friday 11:30 PM the line drops but the Wi-Fi stays up. Cellular was never enabled and there’s no router, so the readers sit on dead Wi-Fi, every tap fails, and 14 rooms and the bar are cash-only, with open tabs that can’t close.
- **Fix:** Enable cellular in every venue’s Terminal Configuration, and show each reader’s network in Admin and the Console. Make a dual-WAN router on a different carrier from the readers a required item (`devices.kind = 'router'`), with a heartbeat that reports wired or LTE and a “venue on backup internet” banner. Test failover monthly. Split the drill into three cases: internet down with Wi-Fi up, access point off, and router LTE off too. Survey cellular signal at the bar, front desk and rooms before go-live.
- **Found by:** the reliability reviewer; the cellular default was re-checked for this review. [Stripe: configure cellular](https://docs.stripe.com/terminal/fleet/cellular), [Stripe: network transitions](https://docs.stripe.com/terminal/features/operate-offline/network-transitions)

### B6 · Offline mode has three writers and a blind replay

- **Where:** Scope and architecture (when the internet drops, step 3); Devices; Tenancy and access (PIN unlock, idle lock); Data model (`idempotency_keys` kept 24 h); the Console’s “Room tablets work offline” beta, on for West 4.
- **Gap:** Staff phones on cellular keep writing to the cloud, including taps and close-outs. The bar computer queues orders and replays them in order against a cloud that has moved on, and with the beta on, room tablets queue too. PINs are checked only in the cloud, so after 3 idle minutes the offline bar computer is stuck behind a PIN pad it can’t unlock, and the bar alarm lives on that computer.
- **What goes wrong:** At 1:10 AM the internet drops and the bartender queues 2 Margaritas for Room 9. At 1:20 a server on cellular closes Room 9 for $498.60 and the party leaves. At 1:45 the replay adds $26 and, by the spec’s own rule, reopens a paid check. Meanwhile guest phones on cellular placed orders that rang only on the offline bar computer; a re-ring plus the replay makes two tickets and two charges.
- **Fix:** Keep one writer: the cloud stays the source of truth, and the bar computer stays online through the router (B5). Anything queued offline is a request, not a write. It gets a client-made `order_id`, deduplicated for 7 days, and is re-validated on replay; it never attaches itself to a finalized or paid check or an earlier business date, and those go to a “Review after outage” list. While offline, the bar computer shows a read-only board and keeps ringing without a PIN. Queuing orders needs an offline code checked on the device itself: a time-based code from a per-device secret set up while online and kept in the OS keychain, or one-time codes printed in advance, valid for that device and business date only. A manager reads it from their phone on cellular or a sealed card; it opens queue mode until the link returns or 4 h pass, every queued order still records a staff name, and it never allows voids, refunds or the drawer. Ring orders on staff phones when the bar computer drops off. Keep the tablet beta off until it follows the same rules.
- **Found by:** the reliability reviewer; the tablet replay also by product. Reliability proposed caching recent PINs on the device for offline unlock and security ruled out storing PIN verifiers there, so this fix takes the stricter line.

### B7 · No recovery targets, one region, and no way to take cards if our cloud is down

- **Where:** Scope and architecture (one US East region, one managed Postgres); Testing and operations (backups, on call); Security and data retention (backups).
- **Gap:** No uptime target, acceptable data loss or recovery time is stated, and the spec doesn’t say whether Postgres or the containers span availability zones. Our cloud going down stops the server-driven readers, cloud printers, tablets and phones together, and a nightly copy to a second region can lose up to 24 h of checks, tips and drawer counts.
- **What goes wrong:** The AWS us-east-1 outage of Oct 20, 2025 ran from 2:48 AM to 5:20 PM Eastern. On this design every venue would have lost the last 72 minutes of Sunday night and the first 80 minutes of Monday’s opening: no tabs, no card close-outs, no tickets.
- **Fix:** Publish targets, for example 99.9% a month for the order, payment and print paths during opening hours; at most 1 min of data loss within the region and 15 min across regions; recovery within 5 min for a zone and 2 h for a region. Run Postgres across two zones, with clients and the event listener riding out a 60–120 s failover, and at least two of each process across zones. Replace the nightly copy with continuous cross-region point-in-time backups, a scripted warm standby, a DNS cutover runbook and a yearly drill. Treat “cloud down” like “internet down” in the desktop app. Add a break-glass way to take cards, such as Tap to Pay in Stripe’s Dashboard app, matched back to checks through the unmatched-payout inbox.
- **Found by:** the reliability reviewer. [AWS event summary](https://aws.amazon.com/message/101925/), [Stripe: in-person payments without code](https://docs.stripe.com/no-code/in-person)

### B8 · Staff PINs on shared screens can be guessed

- **Where:** Tenancy and access (a personal 4-digit PIN on the bar computer, front desk iPad and room tablets; five wrong tries lock for 1 minute); Data model (`memberships.pin_hash`, `failed_pins`); the Pin and AdminDesk screens.
- **Gap:** The PIN pad identifies the person from the PIN alone, so a wrong PIN belongs to nobody and the per-person counter can’t count it; the only limit is 5 tries a minute per device, with no end. Unique PINs can’t be enforced without revealing a colleague’s. A manager PIN on any shared device unlocks manager powers, Admin texts new PINs (so they sit in `messages` and Twilio’s logs), and Argon2id slows each guess but can’t protect 10,000 values offline; only a pepper kept outside the database does.
- **What goes wrong:** A guest alone in Room 9 can type about 250 PINs an hour on the room tablet. With 20–25 staff PINs that’s a 40–47% chance of unlocking as someone within the hour and 78–85% over a 3-hour booking, with about 1 in 5 of hitting one of 3 manager PINs (our estimates). Chosen PINs are worse: 1234, 1111 and 0000 make up about 19% of real-world PINs, so at a 25-person venue one of them is in use about 99% of the time.
- **Fix:** No PIN pad on room tablets: they get kiosk scope, and staff act from their own phones. On shared devices, tap your name, then enter your PIN. Managers and owners use 6 digits; block common and sequential PINs; staff set their own PIN after the invite, and it’s never texted or shown. Lock out per device and per person with growing delays, and after 10 straight failures disable the device until a manager pairs it again. Store `HMAC(pepper, venue ‖ membership ‖ PIN)` before Argon2id, with the pepper in a key service. Manager approvals happen on the manager’s own device (H21).
- **Found by:** the security and product reviewers, independently. [NIST SP 800-63B](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/), [DataGenetics PIN study](http://www.datagenetics.com/blog/september32012/index.html)

## Checked and fine

The reviewers also confirmed these, so they need no change:

- The Room 9 numbers add up on every screen: $322 + $158, $42.60 tax, $96 gratuity, $618.60, less the $120 deposit is $498.60, three shares of $166.20, and $13.46 at 2.7%.
- Integer cents, half-up rounding and largest-remainder splits; the Night screen’s tip shares add up to the cent.
- The Stripe limits as written: 10 increment attempts including declines, Discover limited by merchant category, on-receipt tips up to 50% or $50, whichever is greater, in-person holds of at least 2 days (5 for Visa), and a surcharge that can only go down and can’t follow increments. Partial authorization is online-only (IC+ pricing), so readers never return a partial amount.
- One PaymentIntent per payment, reused after a decline, and “new card first, then cancel the old hold”, which match Stripe’s double-charge guidance.
- Direct charges on each venue’s account through the `Stripe-Account` header, with application fees addable later.
- Webhook de-duplication by event id, a fast 200 and background processing.
- `set_config(…, true)` per transaction fails closed on a reused pooled connection (tested on Postgres 16), and `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` with no BYPASSRLS for the API role is right.
- The 2-minute reader heartbeat and the 15 s–2 min cellular switch for server-driven readers.
- Deferring store-and-forward, since server-driven readers can’t take offline payments.
- The New York values: 4 AM close and 30-minute drinking-up time, 8.875% tax, $17.00 / $11.35 / $5.65 wages, `id_checks` limited to §65-b’s four fields, the “Gratuity” label with 100% to staff, and the 30-day breach deadline.
- Module ids, plan prices and West 4’s $417 a month match across the blueprint and every screen.
- Capacity: a `SKIP LOCKED` queue and 5,000–7,000 sockets are comfortable at 50 venues.
