# Fix the money rules before opening night

West 4 Boho's prototype already does the karaoke-specific work that off-the-shelf bar systems skip: per-person, per-minute room billing, a live 14-room board, in-room ordering that sets off an alarm at the bar, and self-serve booking with deposits. It is not ready to launch, because several money and messaging rules it already applies break New York, card-network or federal rules. The clearest failure is the flat **3.5% card fee**. It is above Visa's and Stripe's **3% caps**, exceeds New York's cost-of-acceptance cap on almost every tab, cannot legally be charged on debit or prepaid cards, and must be posted as a card-inclusive price before purchase. The **automatic 20% gratuity** is lawful only if it is labeled a gratuity, paid in full to eligible service staff (never managers), logged for six years, and sent to payroll as wages, separately from tips guests add themselves. The birthday and review texts need their own written marketing consent, quiet hours and carrier registration, and the single 8.875% tax rate needs a tax category on every line. Five controls are missing outright: a hard **4 AM alcohol stop** with a 4:30 AM clear-out, a cut-off switch for rooms that order by phone, a written offline design, a PCI and data-security baseline, and performing-rights music licenses with Playbox's YouTube playback locked. The designed bar mode also needs payment and alcohol guardrails before it ships. After launch, the highest-value should-haves are the pricing options rivals already sell (before-8 PM room rates, weekend minimums, and packages checked against New York's ban on unlimited drinks), a running bill on the room screen, control of the Playbox player, room-operations tools, ID scanning that stays within the four data fields ABCL § 65-b allows, and safety and license records. Nice-to-haves such as a guest CRM, gift cards, event contracts and OpenTable listings add revenue but not protection. NYC's proposed all-in pricing rule, the Visa/Mastercard settlement and the FCC's January 2027 opt-out rule are all still moving, so price display, surcharge and consent logic should be settings rather than hard-coded, and every item marked for counsel or an accountant needs sign-off before launch.

## Custom room billing already outruns off-the-shelf bar POS

Mainstream bar systems have made tabs, split checks, bar displays, tip pools and QR ordering standard. However, none of the feature documentation reviewed for Toast, Square, Lightspeed, SpotOn, Clover or GoTab describes a timed, per-person room charge; this absence was inferred from the documentation, not tested. A vendor that sells POS software to pool halls says Square and Clover have "zero concept of table sessions," so time-based revenue gets entered by hand as extra line items ([CuePoint, vendor claim](https://www.cuepoint.cloud/blog/best-pos-system-for-pool-halls)). iTab, which sells a karaoke POS from $65 a month, claims "guest timing" and room-level sales but documents no hourly or per-person billing ([iTab](https://www.itabpos.com/karaoke-bars-restaurants-pos/)). Karaoke content platforms go no further than the session. KaraFun Box costs **$199 per room per month** and schedules and locks rooms but has no billing engine, and KaraFun's own guide tells operators to use Toast or Lightspeed for in-room food and drink ([KaraFun Box](https://business.karafun.com/box/); [KaraFun guide](https://business.karafun.com/resources-start-karaoke-business/1223-best-apps-to-run-a-karaoke-box-business.html)). Singa's room booking, announced in April 2026, did not yet take deposits ([Singa](https://singa.com/blog/singa-booking/)). Most NYC rivals still confirm room bookings by hand, through web forms followed by phone or email, or through a Google Form. Only a minority use Square, Roller, TBDine, OpenTable or Resy ([Gagopa](https://www.gagopakaraoke.com/reservation); [Sing Sing](https://www.karaokesingsing.com/reservation); [Karaoke K](https://karaokek.com/)). Rebuilding West 4's scope on Square would start at its $49 or $149 monthly tier plus $20–$30 a month per kitchen or bar display screen, and would still have no room logic ([Square pricing](https://squareup.com/us/en/point-of-sale/restaurants/pricing)).

West 4's commercial terms already sit inside the market range, so the gaps are not in the price list. The **$10 per person per hour** rate matches both Sing Sing locations and Karaoke Boho Orchard's after-8 PM rate. The $250 VIP rate equals Sing Sing Ave A's after-8 PM VIP price. The 20% gratuity sits within the 18–21% rivals charge, and the 15-minute grace period matches the 10–15 minutes rivals hold rooms. The first-hour deposit mirrors Sing Sing Ave A's no-show charge of "1 HOUR times the NUMBER OF GUESTS" ([Sing Sing rooms](https://www.karaokesingsing.com/room); [Sing Sing Ave A](https://www.singsingavea.com/reserve); [Karaoke Boho](https://www.karaokeboho.com/privateroom); [Chorus](https://choruskaraoke.com/reserve/)). The soft end time matches Japanese karaoke-box practice, where staff ring the room 10 minutes before time is up and extend only when the venue isn't busy ([FUN! JAPAN](https://www.fun-japan.jp/en/articles/14102)). The QR waitlist also helps keep the line off the sidewalk, as NYPD asks nightlife venues to do ([NYPD](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nypd/downloads/pdf/publications/best-practices-for-nightlife-establishments-en-2018.pdf)).

The gaps cluster in areas vendors have spent years hardening and the prototype treats as simple settings: New York surcharge rules, offline card handling, gratuity and tip records, inventory, ID checks when alcohol is ordered, and dispute handling. On top of those sit legal duties no vendor carries for a 4 AM Manhattan bar, such as hours of sale, intoxication control, consent for marketing texts, music licensing and fire-code occupancy. Two once-leading products have changed hands: Lightspeed sold Upserve in April 2026, and Shift4 bought Revel in 2024. The benchmarks below therefore use Toast, Square, Lightspeed K-Series, SpotOn, Clover and GoTab ([Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/news/lightspeed-commerce-divests-upserve-u-s-hospitality-product-line-to-skyview-equity-transaction-sharpens-focus-on-core-growth-engines-of-retail-in-north-america-and-hospitality-in-europe/); [Shift4](https://investors.shift4.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/221/shift4-acquires-majority-stake-of-german-point-of-sale-company-vectron-systems-ag-and-completes-previously-announced-acquisition-of-revel-systems)). Each gap lands in one of three tiers. **Must fix before launch** means illegal as configured, or able to break money flows on the first night. **Should have** means needed to match rivals and time-billing systems, or best practice that meaningfully reduces liability. **Nice to have** means it sets West 4 apart but isn't needed to open.

## Five money and messaging rules need fixing before the first tab closes

### The 3.5% card fee fails three separate caps

New York's surcharge law changed on February 11, 2024. General Business Law § 518 now allows a credit-card surcharge only if the seller "clearly and conspicuously post[s] the total price for using a credit card... inclusive of surcharge." It caps the surcharge at "the amount of the surcharge charged to the business by the credit card company" and sets civil penalties of up to $500 per violation ([GBL §518](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/518)). The Department of State requires businesses to display "the highest total price (excluding sales tax)." It also bars separate receipt lines such as "service fee," "technology fee" or "non-cash adjustment" unless the total price was posted first ([NY DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/04/2024-04-10-dos-dcp-credit-card-surcharge-one-page-reference-guide.pdf)).

The card networks and Stripe add their own limits on top. Visa allows no more than the merchant's cost of accepting the card and never more than **3%**. It requires 30 days' notice to the acquirer, disclosure where customers enter and where they pay, and a separate receipt line, and it forbids surcharges on debit and prepaid cards ([Visa](https://usa.visa.com/dam/VCOM/global/support-legal/documents/merchant-surcharging-considerations-and-requirements.pdf)). Mastercard caps surcharges at the lower of the merchant's processing rate or 4% and requires 30 days' notice to both Mastercard and the acquirer. It also bars surcharges on debit and prepaid cards ([Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/business/support/merchant-surcharge-rules.html)). Stripe supports US surcharges only on credit cards and only up to 3%, and it makes the merchant "fully responsible for any fines, penalties, or losses" ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge)).

A party of four for two hours shows how far off 3.5% is. The $80 room charge plus a $16 gratuity and $7.10 tax makes a **$103.10** tab. At Stripe's standard in-person rate of 2.7% plus 5¢, processing that tab costs about **$2.83**, and Visa's 3% cap would allow $3.09. The current fee collects **$3.61**, which is 52¢ over Visa's cap and 78¢ over Stripe's actual cost ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)). At Stripe's in-person rate, a 3.5% fee exceeds actual cost on any domestic ticket above $6.25. At the online rate of 2.9% plus 30¢, it exceeds cost on any charge above $50. A $40 online deposit costs $1.46 to process, but Visa still caps its surcharge at $1.20.

Two routes are compliant: a surcharge on credit cards only, calculated for each transaction, or no surcharge at all. Two tempting shortcuts are closed. Toast suggests a service charge as an alternative to surcharging ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Credit-Card-Surcharging-in-New-York)), but New York presumes any extra "service" charge is a gratuity owed to staff ([12 NYCRR 146-2.18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.18)). A service charge therefore cannot be used to recover card costs. Relabeling the fee as dual pricing does not clearly escape the cost cap either, because § 518 defines two-tier pricing as a credit price "inclusive of any surcharge" posted beside the cash price. Counsel and Stripe should confirm how a cash-discount program treats debit cards.

A surcharge also brings engineering traps. Stripe's surcharge API is still a **public preview** (API version `2026-03-25.preview`). The authorized amount must already include the surcharge, which can be lowered when the payment is captured but never raised. A full refund must return the whole surcharge, and a partial refund a proportional share ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge)). The surcharge also counts as taxable sales ([TSB-A-18(1)S](https://tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/a18_1s.pdf)). The 30-day notice to the card networks is the one launch item with a fixed lead time. [Toast's New York setup](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Credit-Card-Surcharging-in-New-York) is a workable template. Printed menus show prices with the surcharge included ($10.30 for a $10 item at 3%), and receipts show the surcharge on a separate line. Debit and cash payments are never surcharged, and online and QR payments are not surcharged at all, which is a sensible default for West 4's online deposits.

### The 20% gratuity is lawful only if it behaves exactly like a gratuity

New York Labor Law § 196-d bars employers from keeping "any part of a gratuity or of any charge purported to be a gratuity" ([§196-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/196-D)). The Hospitality Wage Order requires such a charge to be "distributed in full as gratuities to the service employees or food service workers who provided the service." It presumes that any extra "service" charge is a gratuity unless the employer proves otherwise. Records of each charge "and their dispositions" must be kept for six years and regularly shown to the staff in the tip pool ([146-2.18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.18)). The same labeling decides sales tax. A mandatory gratuity is exempt only if it is listed separately, explicitly called a gratuity and paid out in full to employees. Using any of it to pay employer costs such as wages makes the whole charge taxable ([TSB-M-09(13)S](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/memos/sales/m09_13s.pdf)). The house can keep a mandatory charge only as an "administrative charge" for a banquet, special function or package deal. That charge must be disclosed on the contract, the menu and the bill in type no smaller than **12-point** ([146-2.19](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.19)).

The hours-based tip pool carries two concrete risks. Employers may require food-service workers to pool tips and may set each occupation's share ([146-2.16](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.16)). But who may share depends on "duties and not titles" ([146-2.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.14)). New York's Court of Appeals shut out anyone with "meaningful authority," such as the power to discipline, evaluate, hire or fire ([Bond Schoeneck & King on *Barenboim*](https://www.bsk.com/new-york-labor-and-employment-law-report/new-york-court-of-appeals-resolves-questions-about-states-tip-sharing-statute)), and federal law likewise bars managers from keeping employees' tips ([US DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/15-tipped-employees-flsa)). The first risk is that a pool counting PIN sign-ins across the Owner, Manager and Staff roles will sweep in managers' hours unless eligibility is set separately for each person. The second is that device sign-ins are not the arrival-and-departure record the payroll rules require ([146-2.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.1)), so hours should come from the time clock. The pool must also keep specific records for six years: a daily log of each employee's cash and card tips per shift, the list of eligible occupations, each occupation's share, and each person's payouts by date ([146-2.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.17)). If the card surcharge also applies to the gratuity, the business should not also deduct card fees from tips. It should skip that deduction even though [146-2.20](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.20) otherwise allows deducting the card fee attributable to a tip.

Federal tax law treats the automatic 20% as a **service charge, not a tip**. The IRS counts a payment as a tip only when the customer freely sets the amount, including zero ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8027)). Payouts from the automatic gratuity are wages subject to withholding and fall outside the 2025–2028 "no tax on tips" deduction of up to $25,000. Only amounts a guest adds on a separate tip line can qualify, and from 2026 those are reported in W-2 Box 12 with code TP ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2026/05/irs-rules-tighten-tip-deduction-eligibility-2026)). The payroll export therefore needs two separate streams. Staff should also hear before launch that their share of the automatic gratuity is taxed as ordinary wages, while tips guests add voluntarily can qualify for the deduction.

Guests object less to automatic gratuity than to surprise. One Boho Orchard reviewer cited "$47" added to a personal portion of $200 ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g60763-d5835028-Reviews-Karaoke_Boho_Orchard-New_York_City_New_York.html)). The Boho brand's own pages also disagree: the reservation page says 18%, and the private-room page says 21% for the VIP room ([reservation](https://www.karaokeboho.com/reservation); [private rooms](https://www.karaokeboho.com/privateroom)). A single gratuity rule, set once and shown identically on the website, confirmation, room screen and receipt, fixes both problems. That means fee amounts should no longer be typed by hand into the free-text website copy in Admin.

### Birthday and review texts are marketing and need their own consent

The booking confirmation, reminder, room code, room-ready, wrap-up and receipt texts are informational. They can go to the number a guest gives at booking, provided the booking page clearly says those texts will be sent ([CTIA](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf)). The birthday offer is marketing, and the next-morning review request is safest treated the same way. Federal rules require **prior express written consent** for marketing texts, including a statement that consent is not a condition of purchase. Opt-outs made by "any reasonable method" must be honored within ten business days. At most one confirmation text may follow an opt-out, and one sent within five minutes is presumed to be covered by the guest's consent. Marketing texts are also barred before 8 a.m. and after 9 p.m. in the guest's local time ([47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)). New York's telemarketing law, GBL § 399-z, explicitly covers "electronic messaging text." It uses the same 8 AM–9 PM window and fines up to **$20,000 per violation** ([GBL §399-z](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/399-Z)).

Carriers and Twilio add another layer. Automated texts sent from an ordinary 10-digit number require Twilio 10DLC brand and campaign registration, and unregistered traffic draws extra carrier fees ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc)). Consent covers "strictly" the subject it was given for ([Twilio policy](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/legal/messaging-policy)). For short-code texts, CTIA treats alcohol content without a working age check as its most severe class of violation ([CTIA handbook](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/CTIA-Short-Code-Monitoring-Handbook-v1.9-FINAL.pdf)). The FCC's "revoke-all" rule, under which one opt-out stops all unrelated messages, now takes effect **January 31, 2027** ([Mac Murray & Shuster](https://mslawgroup.com/delayed-again-fcc-pushes-back-tcpas-revoke-all-rule-to-january-31-2027/)). A federal appeals court struck down its separate one-to-one consent rule ([Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/blogs/fcc-repeals-one-one-consent-rule-following-eleventh-circuit-decision)). The simplest path is to apply every opt-out to every message type now. Liquor law limits the offer itself: the State Liquor Authority (SLA) bans "offerings of free drinks, or multiple drinks for free" ([SLA](https://sla.ny.gov/what-you-need-know-if-youre-licensed-retailer)). The birthday perk should therefore be room time, food or a non-alcoholic item.

### Tax has to follow each line, not the whole tab

New York taxes admission to a place of amusement, and its definition of admission includes "any other charge for entertainment, amusement, or use of the facilities" ([TB-ST-8](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/tg_bulletins/st/admission_charges.htm)). In 2024 the state ruled that escape-room charges are taxable admissions and rejected the argument that staff playing characters made them tax-exempt performances ([TSB-A-24(36)S](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/advisory_opinions/sales/24-36s.htm)). By the same logic, guests singing karaoke are unlikely to count as a tax-exempt musical performance, although no ruling addresses karaoke directly. Taverns owe tax on "all cover, drink minimum, entertainment, or other charges," but not on voluntary tips ([TB-ST-806](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/tg_bulletins/st/sales_by_restaurants.htm)). A fee for paying by card on a taxable sale also counts as taxable sales ([TSB-A-18(1)S](https://tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/a18_1s.pdf)). In a ruling about equipment leases, New York treated a cancellation fee as taxable sales ([TSB-A-91(73)S](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/a91_73s.pdf)). Forfeited deposits should therefore be taxed by default until an accountant says otherwise. No guidance covers damage fees. TB-ST-8 describes admissions tax as 4% state tax plus the local rate, so an accountant should confirm that the combined 8.875% rate is correct for room time.

The record-keeping rule is strict. Guest checks must be numbered in sequence, register tapes must be dated, both must be kept at least three years, and summary reports alone are not enough ([TB-ST-806](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/tg_bulletins/st/sales_by_restaurants.htm)). The prototype's PIN-stamped action log and end-of-night Z report are a strong base. Sequential check numbers that cannot be edited, plus a rule that nothing is ever permanently deleted, would complete it.

### Booking pages must show the whole price and capture the terms

In July 2026, NYC's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) proposed a rule making it deceptive to advertise a price "without disclosing the total price in a clear and conspicuous manner." The rule would also require businesses to disclose the "nature, purpose, and amount" of any fee left out of that price before purchase, with penalties of $525, $1,050 and $3,500 ([DCWP](https://rules.cityofnewyork.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/DCWP-NOH-Rules-Relating-to-Junk-Fees.pdf)). The rule is not final. It covers "mandatory fees" that are "not reasonably avoidable" ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/junk-fees-summer-2026-roundup-part-1-illinois-nyc-and-dc)). The automatic 20% gratuity arguably fits that description, which would make a bare "$10 per person per hour" non-compliant.

Even without the rule, Visa already requires surcharge notice where customers enter, and surprise bills are the complaint rivals hear most. Maru reviewers describe $60-an-hour rooms ending in bills over $500, and gratuity added without consent ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g60763-d4727661-Reviews-Maru_Karaoke_Lounge-New_York_City_New_York.html)). Booking tools in similar industries require guests to accept the terms and cancellation policy before they book ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/tours/karaoke-ticket-booking-software/)). Insa publishes its damage fees item by item, including $100 for confetti, $200 for broken equipment and $150 for bodily-fluid cleanup ([Insa](https://insabrooklyn.com/karaoke)). A stored record of the guest accepting the terms is also the main evidence when a guest disputes a forfeited deposit or a $150 damage charge with their card issuer.

## Alcohol, uptime and licensing need five new controls before opening night

### A hard 4 AM stop must reach every channel that sells alcohol

New York's Alcoholic Beverage Control Law, ABCL § 106(5), bans on-premises alcohol sales from 4 AM to 8 AM Monday through Saturday and from 4 AM to 10 AM on Sunday. It also bars anyone from drinking on the premises "later than one-half hour after the start of the prohibited hours," so **4:30 AM** is the hard end of consumption ([ABCL §106](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABC/106)). The prototype stores opening hours, but nothing in its feature list stops alcohol at 4:00 AM, and three features push the other way. The soft end time lets rooms keep going by the minute. The staff phone sends "let them stay" texts. The designed bar mode closes forgotten tabs automatically at 4 AM.

The server should enforce the cutoff for every alcohol item on every channel, from the room screen and bar tabs to packages and "send the singer a drink." The venue should be able to set an earlier internal last call, and a 4:30 AM check of every room should be logged. The cutoff must run on New York clock time so it still works when daylight saving time changes, while room billing counts minutes actually elapsed. On the November night the clocks fall back, a room booked from 1:30 AM to 2:30 AM by the clock is actually occupied for two hours.

### Phone ordering removes the bartender's eyes, so the system must add them back

Selling alcohol to anyone under 21, or to anyone "visibly intoxicated," is a crime ([SLA](https://sla.ny.gov/frequent-violations-abc-law-retailers)). NYPD notes that the law also forbids "permitting someone else to serve" such a guest, and it tells staff to watch whether "individuals are buying drinks for others who may have been cut off" ([NYPD](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nypd/downloads/pdf/publications/best-practices-for-nightlife-establishments-en-2018.pdf)). New York's dram shop law lets anyone injured by an intoxicated person sue whoever unlawfully sold that person alcohol, for "actual and exemplary damages" ([GOL §11-101](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/11-101)). When a closed room orders through the QR code on its wall, no bartender sees the guest at the moment of sale.

In the prototype, the bar must accept each room order before it is delivered. That is the right checkpoint, and it should stay mandatory for alcohol. What's missing is a way for staff to act on what the runner sees at the door. They need a cut-off, for a whole room or a single guest, that blocks alcohol from every device, including the host's locked ordering. They need a button to refuse an order at delivery. They also need a refusal log that records each decision and links it to the cut-off. No statute requires that log, but it is the best evidence a venue has against a dram shop claim. If West 4 ever admits guests under 21, as Chorus does from 5 to 8 PM and Space does outside Friday and Saturday nights ([Chorus](https://choruskaraoke.com/pricing/); [Space](https://spacekaraoke.com/)), tying the door ID check to who is in each room moves from should-have to must.

### The offline plan needs the decisions every vendor has already made

Every major vendor puts the risk of declined offline payments on the merchant, and each sets explicit limits. Toast switches to offline mode 40 seconds after losing internet ([Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformOfflinePaymentsOverview.html)). It stores card data encrypted on the device and recommends reconnecting within three days, preferably within 24 hours. Owners can cap offline amounts with a manager override, and Toast cannot put a hold on a card while offline ([Toast offline payments](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminOfflineCCPayments.html)). By default its devices also stop talking to each other, so kitchen screens stop showing orders unless a local hub device is set up ([Toast offline mode](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformOfflineMode.html)). Square requires offline payments to be uploaded within 72 hours and lets sellers cap each payment anywhere from $1 to $50,000 ([Square](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/7777-process-card-payments-with-offline-mode)). Clover allows seven days by default ([Clover](https://docs.clover.com/dev/docs/handling-offline-payments)), and SpotOn keeps printers and screens running over the local network ([SpotOn](https://www.spoton.com/blog/pos-offline-mode/)).

West 4's plan has to make the same choices for a venue whose revenue runs on 14 room clocks. It must settle which services the bar computer runs over the local network and what guests ordering from their phones over cellular data see when the cloud cannot reach the bar. It must also set a maximum for any single offline card payment, decide whether card-hold tabs can be opened offline (the benchmarks say no), and define how conflicting edits merge when the connection returns. This research did not verify Stripe Terminal's own offline limits, so check them before the plan is final.

### A custom POS carries its own PCI and data-security duties

Stripe keeps raw card numbers out of the system, but PCI compliance, the card industry's security standard, stays a shared job. The merchant must certify compliance every year, check that webhook messages really come from Stripe, require TLS 1.2 or higher, and keep third-party JavaScript to a minimum ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide)). Merchants using Stripe's card readers certify with the SAQ C questionnaire, or with the shorter SAQ P2PE if they use Stripe's point-to-point encryption option at 5¢ extra per authorization ([Stripe Terminal PCI](https://support.stripe.com/questions/pci-compliance-for-stripe-terminal); [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)). New York's SHIELD Act defines "private information" to include a username or email with its password. It also includes a person's details combined with a driver's license number, a card number with its security code, or a Social Security number. After a breach, the business must notify affected residents, the Attorney General, the Department of State and the State Police within 30 days of discovery ([GBL §899-aa](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-AA); [Data Protection Report](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2025/02/new-york-changes-data-breach-law-in-december-and-february/)). The Act also requires security safeguards sized to the business, from a named security coordinator to deleting data promptly once it is no longer needed ([GBL §899-bb](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-BB)). Floor PINs that lock after five wrong tries are fine for bar devices. The Owner and Admin functions that change prices, issue refunds, or edit the Stripe, Twilio and payroll connections need multi-factor login. Card handling also matters to guests. A Maru reviewer complained that the venue kept their card details, while Boho Orchard tells guests their card number is deleted once the deposit is charged ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g60763-d4727661-Reviews-Maru_Karaoke_Lounge-New_York_City_New_York.html); [Karaoke Boho](https://www.karaokeboho.com/reservation)).

### Karaoke is a public performance even behind a closed door

Copyright law defines a public performance as one "at a place open to the public" ([17 U.S.C. §101](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/101)). A private room rented to the public inside a bar therefore still needs performance licenses. BMI says any business that uses karaoke must get permission from the copyright owners ([BMI](https://www.bmi.com/licensing/restaurant-bar-or-brewery)), and Global Music Rights (GMR) licenses its catalog separately from ASCAP, BMI and SESAC ([GMR](https://globalmusicrights.com/faq)). KaraFun, a typical karaoke content provider, covers only the rights to its recordings and on-screen lyrics, known as sync, mechanical and master rights. It tells venues to get their own ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR licenses ([KaraFun](https://business.karafun.com/helpcenter/278/)). Singa puts infringement exposure at $750 to $30,000 per song, or more if the infringement is willful, and warns that venues cannot play YouTube tracks commercially ([Singa](https://singa.com/blog/usa-karaoke-licensing-for-bars-and-venues-explained-in-5-minutes/)). Playbox advertises **100,000+ songs** and the ability to "play YouTube videos directly through the system," but its site says nothing about licensing ([Playbox](https://www.playboxkaraoke.com/)).

Before opening, West 4 needs licenses from the four performing rights organizations, or PROs (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR), and should evaluate whether it also needs one from a fifth, Alltrack. It also needs Playbox's commercial rights confirmed in writing and YouTube playback locked on every room tablet. The system's part is a license register that tracks renewals and holds the occupancy, karaoke-night and cover-charge figures the PROs use to set their fees.

### The designed bar mode needs guardrails before it ships

The designed card hold that grows with the tab follows GoTab's model. GoTab raises the hold with each order, caps a tab until the card is re-authorized, and stops a guest with an unpaid tab at another GoTab venue from opening a new one. It also texts reminders about unpaid tabs ([GoTab](https://gotab.com/latest/gotabs-loss-prevention-features)). Toast's QR tabs, by contrast, hold only $0.01 ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Setting-Up-Tabs-Pre-Authorization-for-Toast-Mobile-Order-Pay)).

West 4's version faces three constraints. First, Stripe lets a surcharge be lowered but never raised when the payment is captured, so every increase in the hold has to recalculate the surcharge. The research found no Stripe documentation confirming that surcharges work with growing holds, so that combination needs Stripe's confirmation. Second, Toast cannot open card-hold tabs while offline ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Card-Pre-Authorization-FAQs)), and West 4's offline plan should assume the same limit. Third, a QR code on the counter that pays by Apple or Google Pay cannot be the only way to pay. NYC's cashless ban says app-based ordering "does not relieve a business of its obligation to accept cash" when payment happens on the premises ([DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/businesses/CashlessBanGuidance-AppBasedTransactions.pdf)).

Auto-closing forgotten tabs at 4 AM with a default tip raises a separate problem. Toast, for example, adds a 20% tip by default when staff close a waitlist tab ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-With-Toast-Tables-Waitlist-Pre-Authorized-Tabs)). New York treats a charge like that as a gratuity, and federal rules treat it as a service charge. It therefore needs to be disclosed when the tab opens and handled under the gratuity rules above.

The song side has its own rules. Per-song charges are taxable entertainment charges ([TB-ST-806](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/tg_bulletins/st/sales_by_restaurants.htm)). NYC bars typically charge $1–$2 a song with a one- or two-drink minimum, and many still run their queues on post-it notes ([Planet Rose](https://www.planetrosenyc.com/); [Secret NYC](https://secretnyc.co/sing-your-heart-out-top-karaoke-spots-in-nyc/)). For fair rotation, Singa caps each guest at three songs in the queue, and ScanQueue tells singers when they are up next ([Singa](https://help.singabusiness.com/controlling-the-queue-and-song-requests); [ScanQueue](https://scanqueue.com/karaoke-queue-management-system)). Charging "only when the song starts" needs a start signal from Playbox. Its remote app syncs the queue in real time, but the research found no public Playbox management API ([Playbox Remote](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/playbox-remote/id6755547370)). The system also needs its own log linking each song, singer, tab and time, because even Singa's song history leaves out singer names. "Send the singer a drink" must check the recipient's cut-off flag. A "sing to win a drink" promotion would break the free-drink ban. Boho Orchard's reverse offer, "Buy 1 Any Drink Get 1 Free Karaoke Song," does not, because the free item is a song ([Karaoke Boho](https://www.karaokeboho.com/)).

## Twelve should-haves turn a room timer into a venue system

### Rivals already sell cheaper early hours and packages

Downtown rivals discount the early evening, and West 4 is already open then. Boho Orchard charges **$6 per person per hour until 8 PM** and $10 after, and Sing Sing Ave A charges $5 before 8 PM. Time Out reports roughly half off before 8 PM at both Sing Sing locations ([Karaoke Boho](https://www.karaokeboho.com/privateroom); [Sing Sing Ave A](https://www.singsingavea.com/reserve); [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/newyork/bars/best-karaoke-bars)). West 4 opens at 4 PM on weekdays and 2 PM on weekends ([West 4](https://www.west4karaoke.com/)). Rivals also manage weekend demand with minimum party sizes: eight guests at Sing Sing St Marks on weekends and six at Gagopa on Fridays and Saturdays. Sing Sing Ave A won't take Friday or Saturday reservations for groups under six ([Sing Sing](https://www.karaokesingsing.com/reservation); [Gagopa](https://www.gagopakaraoke.com/reservation); [Sing Sing Ave A](https://www.singsingavea.com/reserve)). West 4's own site promises "Minimum 3 People" ([West 4](https://www.west4karaoke.com/)), yet the pricing rules as described include no minimum billable headcount, so a party of two would be billed for only two guests.

Time-billing systems show how to build these features. BilliardPOS sets rates "by day of the week and time band," can pause and resume a table's timer, and splits one table's bill among several players ([BilliardPOS](https://billiardpos.net/en/)). West 4's pricing engine therefore needs rates that change mid-session at 8 PM, separate rules for weekdays, weekends and holidays, and minimum party sizes that vary by day. Because party size drives the bill, headcount changes should be logged with who made them and when; Insa, for example, charges $200 per person over capacity ([Insa](https://insabrooklyn.com/karaoke)). The engine should also let staff pause the clock when equipment fails, as with the broken mic behind one Boho Orchard complaint ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g60763-d5835028-Reviews-Karaoke_Boho_Orchard-New_York_City_New_York.html)).

Packages are the biggest revenue gap. Space sells two-hour bottle packages from **$390 for 10 guests** up to a $5,200 Executive tier, POP's run from $469 to $3,999, and Boho Orchard sells party packages at $24–$80 per person ([Space](https://spacekaraoke.com/); [POP](https://popkaraoke32.com/); [Karaoke Boho](https://www.karaokeboho.com/)). Packages need legal guardrails that the system enforces, rather than leaving them to staff memory. ABCL § 117-a bans selling "an unlimited number of drinks during any set period of time for a fixed price," except at private functions not open to the public ([§117-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABC/117-A)). The SLA also bans free drinks, specials priced below half the regular price, and "a low initial price followed by a price increment per hour" ([SLA](https://sla.ny.gov/what-you-need-know-if-youre-licensed-retailer)). Bars licensed for on-premises drinking cannot sell liquor or wine to be drunk elsewhere, so guests cannot take unfinished bottles home ([ABCL §106](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-york/abc/article-8/106/)). Karaoke City advertises bottomless drinks at "$22 an hour per person" ([Infatuation](https://www.theinfatuation.com/new-york/reviews/karaoke-city)). West 4 should not copy that structure for public rooms without advice from counsel, because it is unresolved whether a room booking can count as a private function. For drink happy hours, Toast's time-based pricing shows the model: it changes prices "automatically based on the time of day or day of the week" ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Building-Happy-Hour-Menus-w-Menu-Specific-Pricing-1493004445781)).

### Guests should watch the bill build

The research found no rival or time-billing product that documents per-minute overage billing. That makes West 4's model distinctive, but it also makes it unfamiliar to guests. The room screen should show a running total. Room time should appear as rate × minutes × headcount, followed by orders, gratuity and tax, and the total by card and by cash. A draft check before close-out and split receipts that show each person's gratuity and tax would complete the picture. Together these answer two complaints about rivals directly: the Boho Orchard gratuity on a "personal portion" and the Maru bills that ran past $500 ([TripAdvisor Boho](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g60763-d5835028-Reviews-Karaoke_Boho_Orchard-New_York_City_New_York.html); [TripAdvisor Maru](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g60763-d4727661-Reviews-Maru_Karaoke_Lounge-New_York_City_New_York.html)).

### The room system should reach the karaoke player

Karaoke platforms tie the player to the booked session. KaraFun's Blocked Mode mutes and locks a room whenever no session is scheduled, so "no one can sneak in extra songs" ([KaraFun](https://business.karafun.com/resources-start-karaoke-business/1548-blocked-mode-lock-down-your-karaoke-rooms-between-sessions.html)). Singa switches a room on when a booking starts, "with no manual code entry needed" ([Singa](https://singa.com/blog/singa-booking/)). Escape-room software shows a countdown on the in-room TV ([ClueMaster](https://www.cluemaster.io/)). Linked to West 4's room clock, those features would stop guests singing for free after close-out or while a room is being cleaned. They would also replace staff walking in with an on-screen 10-minute warning, which matters because staff "interrupting" is a recurring complaint about Maru ([Four Dollar Jacks](https://www.fourdollarjacks.com/bars-near-me/maru-karaoke-lounge)).

Per-room volume caps with a change log help with NYC's noise limit for commercial music. That limit is measured inside neighbors' homes: 42 dB(A) overall, or 45 dB in any band from 63 to 500 Hz, where karaoke bass concentrates ([NYC Admin Code §24-231](https://nycadmincode.readthedocs.io/t24/c02/sch05/)). All of this depends on Playbox exposing controls it does not publicly document, so start that conversation with Playbox early. Room operations complete the picture. Resova, a booking system for escape rooms and activities, builds reset time between bookings into its calendar, according to a comparison written by a competitor ([CaptainBook](https://www.captainbook.io/blog/escape-room-booking-software-7-platforms-compared-%282026%29)). Bowling maintenance tools log each breakdown by lane, equipment, problem and parts used ([MaintBoard](https://maintboard.com/bowling-center-preventive-maintenance)). Logs like these make comps and damage charges easier to defend.

### Safety, ID and license records protect the liquor license

NYPD's nightlife guide warns that "any closed, darkened area presents a potential danger." It recommends keeping incident reports for at least three years and maintaining a list of banned patrons ([NYPD](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nypd/downloads/pdf/publications/best-practices-for-nightlife-establishments-en-2018.pdf)). Reviews of Gagopa mention theft and "safety issues for women" ([Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/477242/%EA%B0%80%EA%B3%A0%ED%8C%8C%EB%85%B8%EB%9E%98%EB%B0%A9)). New York has no official "Ask for Angela" program. That code phrase, which lets a guest ask bar staff for help discreetly, started in the UK and has been adopted voluntarily by some bars elsewhere ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ask_for_Angela)). The existing call-staff button can still offer a discreet "I need help" option. That request should go only to a manager or security, never appear on the room screen, escalate if nobody responds, and open an incident record. Welfare-check timers for each room would address NYPD's warning about closed rooms.

ID scanning is not required, but it protects the venue. ABCL § 65-b gives a licensee a legal defense to a charge of selling to a minor when the guest's ID passed a scan and staff reasonably relied on it. The law also limits stored data to name, date of birth, ID number and expiration date, and bans sharing that data with third parties, "including... marketing" ([§65-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABC/65-B)). NYPD recommends scanning and keeping records for at least 14 days ([NYPD](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nypd/downloads/pdf/publications/best-practices-for-nightlife-establishments-en-2018.pdf)). Toast's WE SCAN integration, the mainstream benchmark, works only online ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Using-WE-SCAN-ID-Scanning-Integration)). Any face-matching feature would trigger NYC's biometric law, which requires signs at the entrance and allows damages of $500 to $5,000 per violation ([Local Law 3 of 2021](https://intro.nyc/local-laws/2021-3)).

NYC no longer licenses cabarets. Local Law 214 of 2017 replaced cabaret licensing with Admin Code § 10-177, which applies to bars and restaurants that hold a liquor license and are zoned as Use Group 12. It requires cameras at every guest entrance and exit, recording at 15 frames per second during business hours and for two hours after closing. Footage must be kept for 30 days, signs must be posted, and proof that the venue's guards are registered must be kept on site ([LL 214](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?GUID=9DEAF056-66FB-4678-8DF3-C9A05000DE81&ID=5710167&M=F)). The 2024 City of Yes zoning overhaul changed how uses are classified, and it is unresolved how that affects the Use Group 12 trigger ([CityLand](https://www.citylandnyc.org/council-approves-city-of-yes-for-economic-opportunity-amendment/)).

The venue's SLA application describes how it operates. Karaoke is a checkbox on that form, and dancing is a separate question. The venue must get the SLA's approval before it departs from what the form says ([SLA](https://sla.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/01/on-premises-application-12192025.pdf)), so a private-party enquiry for a DJ dance night needs a flag. A venue where 75 or more people gather indoors needs a Place of Assembly certificate and an annual FDNY permit. It also needs FDNY-certified safety staff where required and logbooks kept for three years ([DOB](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/dob/project-categories-paco.page); [FDNY](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/fdny/downloads/pdf/business/cof-f03-noe-study-materials.pdf)). The system already knows room capacities and party sizes, so a live headcount, capacity caps and an evacuation roster are cheap additions. The city's Office of Nightlife runs two programs for noise complaints: CURE, which works with venues to fix problems before inspection, and MEND NYC, which mediates between venues and neighbors. It reports that 85% of 2025 CURE referrals avoided inspection and that MEND NYC completed 113 mediations ([ONL](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/sbs/downloads/pdf/about/reports/nightlife-report-2026.pdf)). A complaint log that feeds those programs is inexpensive insurance.

### Back-office depth covers inventory, disputes, labor and tax periods

ABCL § 106(12) requires records kept on the premises of alcohol purchases in gallons, with each supplier's name, license number and address, plus records of sales ([ABCL §106](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-york/abc/article-8/106/)). Toast marks an item sold out automatically when its count reaches zero ([Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminMenuItemInventoryOverview.html)). BinWise compares expected with actual liquor usage to catch over-pouring ([BinWise](https://home.binwise.com/binwise-pro)). Toast's chargeback screen tracks deadlines and evidence and automatically accepts any dispute left past its deadline ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Disputing-Chargebacks-in-Toast-s-Back-End)). Stripe charges $15 per dispute ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing)).

Some New York labor rules hit a 4 AM bar especially hard. Any workday that spans more than ten hours from start to finish earns an extra hour at the basic minimum wage, and a 4 PM–4 AM closing shift qualifies ([146-1.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-1.6)). Staff sent home on a slow night are owed call-in pay of at least three hours, or their regular shift if it is shorter ([146-1.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-1.5)). In 2026, NYC employers can pay tipped food-service workers $11.35 an hour and count $5.65 of tips toward the $17.00 minimum wage, a tip credit they lose on any day a worker spends more than two hours, or more than 20% of the shift, on non-tipped work ([NYS DOL](https://dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage-tipped-workers)). NYC's Fair Workweek scheduling law, by contrast, covers fast-food chains and retail, not an independent bar ([DCWP](https://nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/workers/FAQs-FairWorkweek-FastFood.pdf)). Tax reports should follow New York's sales-tax quarters: March–May, June–August, September–November and December–February. They should also flag when sales reach $300,000 in a quarter, the level that triggers monthly filing ([NY filing requirements](https://www.ny.gov/sites/default/files/atoms/files/FilingRequirementsForSalesUseTax.pdf)).

## Nice-to-haves can wait, but moving rules and open questions cannot

The nice-to-have tier covers what rivals and specialist platforms already sell. None of it is urgent, but each item adds revenue or polish once the basics comply with the law. For events, Tripleseat runs proposals, e-signatures, deposit schedules and guest menu choices in one portal ([Tripleseat](https://tripleseat.com/features/guest-portal/)), and CenterEdge times party food to the booking ([CenterEdge](https://centeredgesoftware.com/products/advantage-events/event-bookings/)); both fit West 4's VIP room and private-party enquiries. Booking tools sell gift vouchers as standard ([Resova](https://get.resova.com/features/feature/all-features/)), and Golf O'Clock automates punch cards and prepaid blocks of hours ([Golf O'Clock](https://golfoclock.com/)). Toast Guestbook and SevenRooms show what a guest database with VIP tags and spend history looks like ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Access-Your-Guest-Data-with-the-Guest-Report); [SevenRooms](https://sevenrooms.com/nightclubs-bars/)). Because ABCL § 65-b bars marketing use of ID-scan data, any such database must be kept separate from scan records. Maru, Insa and RPM take bookings through OpenTable, Resy or Litty ([OpenTable Maru](https://www.opentable.com/r/maru-karaoke-lounge); [Resy Insa](https://resy.com/cities/new-york-ny/venues/insa-karaoke-room)), and KaraFun Box adds battle and quiz modes ([KaraFun Box](https://business.karafun.com/box/)). New York already requires the notice "THIS PRICE WAS SET BY AN ALGORITHM USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA" next to personalized prices ([Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/new-york-requires-disclosures-for-personalized-pricing.html)), and a ban on pricing based on personal data has passed both houses of the legislature ([S8623B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B)).

## Conclusion

The research turns the usual build-versus-buy worry upside down. Off-the-shelf systems come with compliant payment handling and no concept of a timed, per-person room, while West 4's prototype has the room logic but copied its money rules from common market practice. Most launch fixes are therefore labeling and configuration rather than new modules. The schedule is set by the items that depend on outside parties: the 30-day surcharge notice to the card networks, carrier registration for texts, performing-rights licenses and Playbox's cooperation. Those should start first. Two findings need an owner's decision rather than a patch. The first is whether to keep a surcharge at all. On the worked tab a compliant surcharge recovers about $2.83, but it brings debit-card checks, network notices, proportional refunds and dual price displays. A price increase of about the same size would recover the cost with none of that overhead, at the price of charging cash payers more. The second is how to tell staff that their share of the automatic gratuity is taxed as ordinary wages, while tips guests choose to add qualify for the federal deduction, a difference that shapes how staff will view the 20% model.

Most of the remaining compliance work is cheap, because the prototype already models the right things. Rooms have states, tabs have clocks, every action carries a PIN, and every alcohol order already passes through a person at the bar. A cut-off flag, a refusal log, welfare timers, an evacuation roster and an incident record are new states and fields on those existing parts, not new systems. The features most at risk of being streamlined away are the ones already doing compliance work: bar acceptance of room orders, the QR waitlist and the PIN-stamped log. They should stay. The expensive, outside-dependent work sits in two places, the Playbox integration (no public API was found) and a real offline design, and both should be scoped now. If surcharge, gratuity, tax and consent rules are built as settings from the start, the answers from counsel and the accountant will change settings rather than code.
