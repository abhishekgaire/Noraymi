# Keep every control, cut the detours

Toast, Square, Clover, Lightspeed, TouchBistro, SpotOn and Aloha all give staff the same machine: a grid of menu buttons beside the check, PIN or card sign-in, tabs secured by a card hold that takes the guest's name from a chip dip, and tips keyed in one payment at a time. None publishes tap or timing data, so what separates them, and what hurts at peak, is where money moves: naming a tab when a guest taps a phone, holds that never grow, tabs and tips closed one at a time, and approvals that stop the bartender until a manager walks over. Toast allows **one pre-authorized card per check with no incremental authorization** and cannot close those checks or adjust their tips in bulk, while Square made bar-tab holds generally available only around **May 2025**, fixes them at a $1.01–$250 floor or the cart total and, like Toast, makes staff type a name for every tapped card. Karaoke systems in Japan, China and Korea add what bar systems lack: a room board whose tiles show state, countdown and a **ten-minute warning**, one-tap extensions, service-call queues, and room moves and merges, although no system anywhere documents per-minute billing. A busy New York bar is an **81-dBA**, dim, wet, constantly interrupted workplace where about **half the staff quit each year**, and West 4's 14 rooms add a second queue of bills that grow by the minute behind doors where no one hears an alarm. The design that fits keeps every control but turns each one into a visible state on the tab or room tile (hold headroom, ID checked, cut-off, minutes left, approval pending) and removes second visits: role home screens, badge-tap sign-in, a static grid with a one-tap repeat round, card-first tabs named by room or seat, holds that follow the clock, a room-order queue instead of an alarm, tips on the guest's screen, one reviewed bulk close after last call, approvals pushed to a manager's phone, and local-first operation that survives outages. That design beats Toast and Square at eleven documented seams, but only once West 4 matches their payment reliability, since freezes and lost tabs top the complaints about every vendor; and because no published benchmark exists, every speed target here is a hypothesis to time in the venue.

## Order screens have converged; tabs, tips and approvals have not

The best evidence on how these systems treat staff is their own help documentation, because no vendor publishes measured tap counts or task times and no independent timing study exists. Marketing adds little: Toast's bar and nightclub page lists features but names no bar customers and offers no bar performance figures ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/restaurant-pos/bar-pos)). Read side by side, the help documents describe one shared design. What differs is how many steps sit between the guest and the money, and who has to walk where when something needs approval. Clover's official help center refused automated access, so its entries rest on developer documentation, resellers and Clover's feature-request forum. Square names a "bar mode" without documenting how its screens differ. Tap counts in this section are estimates built from documented steps, not measurements.

### Order entry is a configurable grid, and bar-speed features are rare

Toast organizes the order screen as menus, then groups, then item buttons, with a search bar and an Open Item button above the grid, the check on the left, and Send, Stay and Hold appearing once items are added ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/New-POS-Experience-Ordering-Screens)). Its one bar-specific recommendation is **Open View**, which lays menus out in color-coded columns with modifiers beside the items rather than on sequential screens; Toast recommends it for "cafes, bars/breweries, food trucks" ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-Open-View)). Every other speed lever is configuration. Buttons stay white until a manager colors them ([Toast Platform Guide](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminAssigningPOSButtonNicknameColorinMenuBuilder.html)), "NO" and "EXTRA" are two-tap pre-modifiers ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Pre-Modifiers-1493068611745)), and layouts are set per device rather than per person. Staff can repeat a single item, but Toast documents no whole-round repeat, personal favorites or item-code entry, and its documentation describes no separate bar mode. Built from the documented layout, two bottles of beer take about four taps and a vodka soda on the rocks five or six. The new POS also moved Split into an overflow menu ([Toast FAQ](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/New-POS-FAQ)).

Square's unified app, launched in April 2025, offers seven modes including a bar mode, but "You cannot combine features from multiple modes into one," and Square says the food-and-beverage modes are not available on iPhones or Android phones and tablets ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8458-use-modes-with-square-point-of-sale)). The launch release credits bar mode with "smart pre-authorization features with conversational modifier inputs" ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250424014995/en/Square's)), yet no help document says what it changes on screen, and Square's headline metric, beta sellers using features "nearly 80% higher," measures adoption rather than speed ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/point-of-sale/2025/square-launches-customizable-unified-pos-app/)). Square's documented strengths are a drag-and-drop tile grid with colors, sizes, images and pages ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/7804-organize-your-menu-with-square-for-restaurants)) and automatic progression through required modifier sets. Its conversational "Add", "No" and "Extra" buttons cannot be combined with modifier quantities, which rules out "2x lime" ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5119-create-and-manage-item-modifiers)). Clover's developer documentation confirms that its modifier dialog opens automatically when an item is added and enforces minimum and maximum choices ([Clover Developer Docs](https://docs.clover.com/dev/docs/managing-modifier-groups-modifiers)). Lightspeed's K-Series keeps a keypad with "Open a tab" and "Quickpay" visible during direct sales, ties menus to device profiles and can make single buttons manager-only ([Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050328394-Understanding-the-Register-screen); [Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804605410-Managing-menus)).

Documented features built for bar speed are rare. TouchBistro's **Fast Bar** puts green cash-amount buttons on the register that close a sale in one tap and reset the screen with the change due. It gets there partly by skipping required modifier prompts for drinks with a default course and leaving upcharges to a long-press, which assumes the bartender remembers them ([TouchBistro](https://cdn.touchbistro.com/help/articles/fast-bar)). Aloha lands anyone clocked in as a bartender on its "Working with Tabs" screen, offers Repeat Item, turns items from blue to black once sent and shows Fast Cash change "in a large grey box" ([Aloha Jobcodes](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/field_definitions/jobcodes); [Aloha FOH guide](https://proddocsitesa.blob.core.windows.net/downloads/aloha-pos/TS_FOHCashierGuide.pdf)). The only documented whole-round repeat belongs to Oracle Simphony, outside this set: one button re-orders the previous round's items that are flagged as repeatable, and the whole action stops if any of them has run out ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/F32325_01/doc.192/f32328/c_menu_items_repeat_rounds.htm)). Staff reviews praise outcomes such as "easy" and "find everything quickly" far more than named features. The micro-features staff do mention are bulk actions and gestures: Aloha's multi-select duplicate, TouchBistro's swipe to remove, and a requested "next seat" button on Toast handhelds ([Capterra Aloha](https://www.capterra.com/p/178748/Aloha/reviews/); [Capterra TouchBistro](https://www.capterra.com/p/140677/TouchBistro/reviews/); [G2 Toast](https://www.g2.com/products/toast/reviews)).

### Tabs break at the name, the hold and the close

Every system that names tabs from cards does it from a chip dip or a swipe, and every one that mentions contactless admits the tap brings no name. Toast says "tapping requires manual tab name entry" ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Starting-a-Tab-1492811100378)). Square falls back to "card type and last four digits" when no name can be read ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8455-enable-and-configure-preauthorization-for-bar-tabs)). Lightspeed tells staff to avoid contactless altogether ([Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408089985179-Creating-and-managing-bar-tabs)), and SpotOn shows a separate naming prompt ([SpotOn](https://spotonteam.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SK/pages/3402891265/Start+a+Tab+FAQs+World+Pay+SpotOn+Restaurant+FOH)). Every tapped card or phone therefore becomes a typing task at the rail.

The hold is where the designs diverge most. Toast permits **one pre-authorized card per check**, lets the hold expire after 24 hours and "cannot currently send multiple increments as the tab grows," so large tabs decline at close; its advice is to ring the first order before taking the card or to raise the default hold ([Toast FAQ](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Card-Pre-Authorization-FAQs); [Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Preauthorized-Cards-Decline-When-Closing)). Square's holds reached general availability around May 2025, after bar owners had waited about two years and threatened to move to Toast ([Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Square-for-Restaurants/CC-Pre-Authorization-for-bar-tabs/td-p/652865)). A Square hold is the larger of a configured $1.01–$250 minimum or the cart total, lasts about 36 hours, and can leave guests looking at "2-3 pending transactions" ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8455-enable-and-configure-preauthorization-for-bar-tabs)). Clover documents that capturing more than 20% above a hold is not guaranteed and that debit holds can lapse within 1–8 business days ([Clover Developer Docs](https://docs.clover.com/dev/docs/transaction-types)). Its request for incremental holds in Clover Dining, filed in 2022, has been marked "Started" since April 2025 ([Clover UserVoice](https://clover.uservoice.com/forums/941725-taking-payments/suggestions/45964759-clover-dining-add-incremental-authorizations)). Lightspeed's K-Series lets a tab run past its hold and flags it with a yellow badge, while its O-Series says the hold "cannot be exceeded" and allows one open tab per card ([Lightspeed K](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408089985179-Creating-and-managing-bar-tabs); [Lightspeed O](https://o-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/31329444176539-Setting-up-bar-tabs-with-Lightspeed-Payments)). SpotOn holds the larger of a preset or the first order ([SpotOn](https://spotonteam.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SK/pages/3402891265/Start+a+Tab+FAQs+World+Pay+SpotOn+Restaurant+FOH)) and advertises incremental authorization ([SpotOn blog](https://www.spoton.com/blog/bar-tabs-pre-authorization-speed-up-transactions-eliminate-walkouts/)). GoTab, a nightlife system outside this set, raises the hold with each order and caps a tab until the card is re-authorized ([GoTab](https://gotab.com/latest/gotabs-loss-prevention-features)).

The close is where Toast's limits bite hardest. Pre-authorized checks "will not close automatically," bulk actions exclude "Closing to saved card, printing, and tip adjustments," and combining two checks drops their holds, so staff must take a new payment method ([Toast FAQ](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Card-Pre-Authorization-FAQs); [Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Bulk-Check-Management); [Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Combining-Checks-1492892550423)). Toast's restaurant-wide auto-close runs at the business-day cutoff, **4 a.m. by default**, and books unpaid checks to an "Auto-Close" payment counted as revenue; moving the cutoff requires Customer Care ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Enable-Auto-Close-or-Auto-Void-for-Check-Reconciliation)). For a bar that sells until 4 a.m., that default lands on closing time. Square documents no bulk close either. Its Close of Day warns about open checks but can be forced with "Run Anyway" and a manager code, a button one bartender on Square's forum did not know existed ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6566-customize-your-closing-procedures-with-square-for-restaurants); [Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Square-for-Restaurants/Best-Practice-for-handling-Unpaid-Bar-Tabs/m-p/748926/highlight/true)). Finding a tab later means searching by name, table or check number, or re-reading the card, in Toast ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Finding-Checks)); sorting by last name in Square ([Square blog](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/inside-square/mastering-bar-tabs)); searching by cardholder name or tab identifier in Lightspeed ([Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408089985179-Creating-and-managing-bar-tabs)); or sorting by name or age in Aloha Cloud ([Aloha Cloud](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-cloud/using/working_with_tables_and_tabs)). None of the documents reviewed describes a search by the card's last four digits.

### Tips and cash still take a second pass

Toast's payment screen is the best-documented close for a held card. A large green "Saved CC" bar showing the name and last four closes the check without the card, the guest's tip screen highlights the highest suggestion with Custom and No tip below it, and "No receipt" comes first ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/New-POS-Managing-Payments)). For cash, quick buttons suggest round amounts, such as "$21, $25, or $30 for a $20.43 check," and an optional Fast $$ button closes a sale in one tap ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Taking-Payments)). Paper-slip tips are a second visit. Staff open Closed Checks, pick the check, tap Pay, retype the tip, then tap Update and Done, one check at a time ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Adjust-Tips-on-a-Closed-Check)), and payments captured after the nightly closeout "cannot be updated with tips" ([Toast Platform Guide](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminOfflineCCPayments.html)). One owner reported that during a power outage staff could not update tips before the 4 a.m. batch ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=4)).

Square asks for the tip before payment by default and uses "smart" amounts: $1, $2 or $3 under $10, and 15%, 20% or 25% above that ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8631-set-up-and-customize-tipping)). It can settle paper tips in bulk, but tips on signed checks must be entered within 36 hours or the payments settle with a $0 tip, and by default "Team members can only settle payments they've processed" ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8375-settle-payments-and-manage-tips)). Its cash flow opens a connected drawer on Tender ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5177-accept-cash-checks-and-other-tender)) but documents no quick-cash buttons. The other systems show the same second pass. Clover makes merchants choose on-screen or paper tipping, not both, and a Canadian reseller notes that a tip not entered before the batch closes is lost ([Clover UserVoice](https://clover.uservoice.com/forums/964475-payments/suggestions/49456676-tips-on-screen-or-print-out-on-paper); [Optimum Payment](https://www.optimumpayment.ca/en/blog/setting-up-tips-on-a-clover-terminal-what-to-check-before)). Lightspeed edits tips one payment at a time, only within the business day, and captures held cards the next morning ([Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4415116773403-Handling-tips-and-gratuities)). TouchBistro's legacy batch runs at 11:30 p.m. Eastern ([TouchBistro](https://cdn.touchbistro.com/help/articles/using-tb-payments/)), and one of its servers called tip entry "cumbersome, wastes time" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/touchbistro-restaurant-pos/reviews)).

### Sign-in and approvals trade speed against accountability

Sign-in designs run from fast and anonymous to personal and slow. Square's shared team passcode "does not support the ability to track time, sales, or activity by team member," while its personal 4-digit passcodes can be required "After each sale" or after a timeout ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8357-require-passcodes-at-point-of-sale); [Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Square-for-Restaurants/How-to-turn-on-require-a-passcode-after-each-transaction-setting/m-p/801117)). Toast pairs passcodes with a Toast Access Card that can replace them, a screen timeout as short as five seconds, and a per-device "Primary Mode" that sets the screen after login ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Assign-a-Swipe-Card-to-an-Employee-Manager); [Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Adjust-POS-Screen-Timeout); [Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Setting-Up-the-New-POS-Experience)). Lightspeed accepts a 4-digit PIN or a personal QR code ([Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804647209-Managing-POS-users)). SpotOn lets staff swipe a card through the payment terminal to sign in or approve, although "Manager cards are not encrypted" ([SpotOn](https://spotonteam.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SK/pages/5220073473)), and Clover's Station Solo includes a thumbprint reader ([Tech.co](https://tech.co/pos-system/clover-pos-pricing)). Aloha documents the most complete shared-terminal kit: magnetic cards, fingerprints, Dallas Keys or RFID badges; a default screen for each terminal and each job; "Automatically exit on close check"; and a "rapid-access" login that lets two bartenders work one cash drawer ([Aloha](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/field_definitions/employees); [Aloha](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/field_definitions/terminals); [Aloha](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/field_definitions/jobcodes)). That depth has a cost: Forbes calls Aloha's feature set "overwhelming at first" ([Forbes Advisor](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/business/aloha-pos-review/)).

Approvals are almost always in-line. When a Toast employee lacks a permission, the same device asks for "a manager passcode or swipe" ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Bulk-Check-Management)). Square asks for a passcode or permission to move another server's check and a reason for every comp or void ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8166-comp-void-and-reassign-checks-with-square-for-restaurants)), and it needs the owner passcode to override closing procedures ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6566-customize-your-closing-procedures-with-square-for-restaurants)). Aloha requires a manager for "most" comps and promotions ([Aloha](https://proddocsitesa.blob.core.windows.net/downloads/aloha-pos/TS_FOHCashierGuide.pdf)). Only SpotOn's brochure advertises a Mobile Manager app for "remote approvals and voids" ([SpotOn](https://spoton-prod-websites-user-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/static/uploads/files/Lfe57MiQeSmEpYJMJ50C_SpotOn%20Restaurant%20POS%20%281%29.pdf)). Reviews show permissions miscalibrated in both directions. An entertainment-venue GM reported that Toast let team members "observe manager passwords and easily exploit" them ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/)). A 2026 reviewer argued that changing a seat number should not require "Edit Sent Items" access and reported that the Go 3 swipe card "only works on handhelds OR terminals, not both" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/toast/reviews)).

### Handhelds draw the most praise and the most connectivity complaints

Handhelds are the feature staff praise most in the Toast reviews sampled, for entering orders "in real-time" and letting guests pay without waiting ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/)). They also draw complaints that the "connection drops easily" while staff move, and that units go back on warranty ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/toast/reviews)). Toast's Go 3, announced in July 2025, adds cellular fallback to a 6.52-inch, 426-gram device ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/new-toast-go-3-handheld-with-cellular-connectivity-toastiq); [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/why-we-built-toast-go)), though the handheld layout pushes Split and Discount into overflow menus ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/New-POS-Experience-Ordering-Screens)). Square Handheld costs $399, requires Wi-Fi and takes offline payments for up to 24 hours ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/hardware/handheld)); it has no built-in printer ([Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/reviews/square-handheld-pos/)), and its pay flow adds "Review sale" and "Confirm & Pay" steps ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8499-accept-payments-with-square-handheld)). Every speed claim for handhelds, such as Toast's "15–20% faster" table turns and a SpotOn survey in which 58% of operators reported faster service, comes from the vendors ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-handheld-pos); [SpotOn](https://www.spoton.com/blog/servers-guide-handheld-pos/)). Offline behavior is bounded too. Toast switches to offline mode after about 40 seconds and keeps ordering and card payments going, but staff cannot close checks or declare cash tips, and Toast tells them "Do not log out" ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Using-Toast-in-Offline-Mode)); it also cannot place card holds offline ([Toast Platform Guide](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminOfflineCCPayments.html)). Square requires offline payments to be uploaded within 72 hours under a per-payment cap of $1 to $50,000 ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/7777-process-card-payments-with-offline-mode)). Lightspeed K-Series falls back to a standalone terminal whose sales staff re-enter by hand ([Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/5028210082715-Standalone-payments)).

## Karaoke systems run the house from a room board with a ten-minute warning

Karaoke operators in Japan, China and Korea solved a problem bar vendors never faced: selling time in closed rooms. Their front-desk screen is a map, not a menu. Anest's Japanese karaoke-box system opens on a room layout whose positions, colors and sizes staff arrange freely, with **up to 100 rooms on a page** ([Anest](https://anest-n.co.jp/karaoke.html)). The waiter app of K米 (KTVme), a Chinese KTV platform, opens on room status, and staff tap a room to open it, take orders, split payment, check out or answer a room call ([K米](https://open-doc.ktvme.com/read/fwydd/kftp?wd=%E9%85%8D%E7%BD%AE)). Chinese cashier suites treat opening, changing and merging rooms (开房, 转房, 并房) and drink returns as core functions ([乾元坤和](https://www.qykh2009.com/sol_help_3.html)), and Japan's Meniu-kun marks a room "bashing complete" (バッシング完了) once it has been cleared ([メニウくん](https://www.meniu-kun.com/lineup/karaoke/)). Each tile therefore carries a state (vacant, reserved, in use, cleaning), a clock, and whatever alerts the room has raised.

The clock drives the rules. Anest lights a red LED when **10 minutes remain** and flashes it when time runs out, warns guests with audio, and takes extension requests from the in-room terminal, which the front desk can block when the venue is busy ([Anest](https://anest-n.co.jp/karaoke.html)). Most Japanese venues phone the room 10 minutes before the end ([FUN! JAPAN](https://www.fun-japan.jp/en/articles/14102)). Chains sell extensions in fixed steps, mostly 30 minutes at Manekineko and Jankara and 15 at Round One, and some extend automatically, which bills guests who linger ([Hatena](https://dokodeyomu.hatenadiary.com/entry/2026/05/04/211457)). Chinese self-service KTV reminds guests about 10 minutes before the end, lets them pay to extend, recalculates fees automatically and cuts the power after a delay the manager sets; one vendor claims the design halves staff costs ([Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/866156648_121950269)). Another locks the equipment until a renewal is paid ([Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/900024466_122435047)). Korean noraebang go the other way, customarily adding 10–20 free minutes of "service time" (서비스 시간) near the end of an hour, though rarely on busy weekend nights ([Namu Wiki](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%85%B8%EB%9E%98%EB%B0%A9)). Billing modes run from minimum spend to hourly fees, buyouts and per-person versions of each ([乾元坤和](https://www.qykh2009.com/sol_help_3.html)), rooms open pay-first or pay-later ([K米](https://open-doc.ktvme.com/read/fwydd/kf)), and prices vary by day and hour ([Anest](https://anest-n.co.jp/karaoke.html)). No source in any market showed **per-minute room billing**.

Ordering moved into the room because the intercom failed. A Japanese karaoke critic wrote in 2014 that food ordered at check-in never arrived because busy staff forgot intercom orders, and she called for terminals that send orders straight to the kitchen ([enjoysing.com](https://enjoysing.com/karaoke1750)). JOYSOUND was rolling out a touch-screen order terminal to stores nationwide by 2017 ([JOYSOUND](https://shop.joysound.com/campaign/chumonkun/)). Jankara now lets guests order one-handed from their own phones while singing, pre-order for a reserved slot, and settle reserved rooms with "0-second checkout" ([Jankara](https://jankara.ne.jp/pr/apporder/)). Anest routes room orders to up to eight printers by category ([Anest](https://anest-n.co.jp/karaoke.html)). Service calls are kept separate from orders and sent over more than one channel. Meniu-kun builds staff calling into the order terminal and flashes repeated calls ([メニウくん](https://www.meniu-kun.com/lineup/karaoke/)). K米 pushes calls to its waiter app but warns that on iOS the custom alert sound plays only when the app is in the background ([K米](https://open-doc.ktvme.com/read/fwydd/hjsz)). Off-the-shelf kits pair room buttons with watch or display receivers; Retekess's T114 supports up to 999 buttons and six prompt modes ([Retekess](https://www.retekess.com/products/t114-wireless-calling-system)).

American practice is thinner. US rooms are usually priced by the room per hour, with a phone or call button for ordering from the bar ([FindKaraoke](https://findkaraoke.net/blog/private-karaoke-rooms)); Karaoke City NYC charges $50 an hour for up to five people plus $10 an hour for each additional guest ([Karaoke City](https://karaokecitynyc.com/reserve-a-room/)). iTab, which has a Manhattan office, claims room bookings, "guest timing" and room-level sales, with handhelds in rooms and QR orders routed to the bar or kitchen, but it documents no timer mechanics ([iTab](https://www.itabpos.com/karaoke-bars-restaurants-pos/)). KaraFun's Blocked Mode mutes and locks a room's player whenever no session is scheduled ([KaraFun](https://business.karafun.com/resources-start-karaoke-business/1548-blocked-mode-lock-down-your-karaoke-rooms-between-sessions.html)). The research found no public documentation for Korean noraebang counter software, or for how any KTV system handles staff sign-in and approvals, so those parts of West 4's design have to come from bar systems. West 4 charges **$10 per person per hour with a three-guest minimum** ([West 4](https://www.west4karaoke.com/)), and its prototype bills by the minute after the first hour. That model has no documented precedent. It is an edge over rivals, and it also means staff will spend checkout explaining the bill unless the screen explains it for them.

## 81-decibel bars and 50% annual quits set the design limits

### The room defeats sound, small targets and long sequences

Noise rules out sound as the main alert channel. A survey of 2,376 New York venues found bars averaging **81 dBA** at prime time, with 60% rated "Very Loud" and another 31% "Loud," and people with normal hearing struggle to follow a conversation above 75 dBA ([Scott 2018](https://file.scirp.org/Html/5-1761957_86590.htm)). Commercial karaoke rooms averaged above **95 dBA**, with peaks beyond 115 dBA ([Int. J. Industrial Ergonomics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169814103000234)). Fire alarms must sound 15 dBA above ambient to be heard ([EC Magazine on NFPA 72](https://www.ecmag.com/magazine/articles/article-detail/proper-audibility-level-how-to-determine-fire-alarm-volume-before-the-building-is-built)). By that yardstick a bar alert would need about 96 dBA and one inside a karaoke room about 110 dBA, beyond NIOSH's limit of 15 minutes a day at 100 dBA ([OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/noise)). Spoken confirmations between staff fail for the same reason. Light is scarce too: three-quarters of the restaurants in one survey measured below about 14 lux at the plate ([IES](https://ies.org/lda/the-secret-recipe-for-restaurant-lighting-2/)). Yet dark text on a light background still read better at night in glance tests ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)), which leaves a real tension between glare and legibility that only testing in the venue can settle.

Hands and bodies set the next limit. Water on a capacitive screen registers phantom touches and shifts the touch point; selecting a target on a wet phone took **2.13 attempts and about 2.5 seconds**, against 1.11 attempts and half a second with a software fix ([Tung et al. 2018](https://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/icmi-18.01.pdf)). Standing users picked screen tilts between 30° and 55° in 92% of cases, with no single best angle ([Schultz et al. 1998](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169814197000875)). People walking made the fewest errors on **14-millimeter** buttons, and total errors rose from 18 standing to 47 walking ([Conradi et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2351978915001833)). Research puts reliable single taps at 9.2–9.6 mm ([Parhi et al. 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/01/parhi-mobileHCI06.pdf)) and corner targets at about 12 mm ([Hoober](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php)). Both exceed the platform minimums: Apple's 44 points is about 8.5 mm on an iPad ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)), and Android's 48 dp is about 7.6 mm ([Android](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)).

Attention is the third limit. Interruptions averaging 2.8 seconds **doubled** errors in a seven-step procedure and 4.4-second interruptions **tripled** them, specifically by making people skip or repeat steps ([Altmann et al. 2014](https://interruptions.net/literature/Altmann-JExpPsycholGen14.pdf)); a bartender asked a question mid-order is exactly that subject. Stable layouts beat clever ones. Static menus were faster than menus users customized, which were faster than menus the system reordered on its own, and participants found the self-reordering menus frustrating ([Findlater & McGrenere 2004](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/th/2004/Findlater2004/Findlater2004.pdf)). Tap latency below about 20 ms goes unnoticed, while delays around 64–69 ms are noticed ([Jota et al. 2013](https://www.tactuallabs.com/papers/howFastIsFastEnoughCHI13.pdf); [Deber et al. 2015](https://www.tactuallabs.com/papers/howMuchFasterIsFastEnoughCHI15.pdf)). About 1 in 12 men has a color-vision deficiency, so red and green alone cannot carry status ([NEI](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness)).

### The crew turns over faster than training can keep up

US accommodation and food services lost **4.1–4.2% of their workers to quits each month** in 2024 and 2025, roughly half the workforce a year, against 2.2–2.3% for the private sector as a whole ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.t22.htm)). 7shifts puts average tenure at 110 days and the cost of replacing a front-of-house worker at $1,056 ([7shifts](https://www.7shifts.com/blog/true-cost-of-employee-turnover/)). More than 60% of New York City restaurant workers were immigrants in 2018, 44% Hispanic and 20% Asian ([NY State Comptroller](https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/reports/osdc/pdf/nyc-restaurant-industry-final.pdf)), and Chinese and Korean are among the city's ten designated languages ([NYC MOIA](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/immigrants/downloads/pdf/CY2021-local-law-30-report.pdf)). Owners say new staff learn Toast "in 1 day" and Lightspeed in "10 minutes" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=4); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/211849/Lightspeed-Resturant/reviews/)), while a TouchBistro GM called onboarding "horrendous" and ended up writing a manual ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140677/TouchBistro/reviews/)). The quick-learning claims most likely describe basic order entry, since complaints cluster in splits, tips, voids and permissions, the edge cases training rarely covers. The strongest evidence on skill comes from tabletop ordering, where the slowest servers cut meal times by 13.88% against 6.19% for the fastest ([Tan & Netessine](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/2017.9.10_Tabletop_For_Submission_WithNames.pdf)). Good tools help the weakest staff most, and in a crew that turns over by half each year, that describes a large share of the crew.

Sign-in cost compounds with turnover. In the closest published analog, a smartphone PIN unlock took **4.9 seconds**, against 2.6 seconds for a slide with no secret ([Harbach et al. 2014](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/soups2014/soups14-paper-harbach.pdf)). A bartender who enters 40–60 orders an hour and retypes a 3–5-second PIN for each would spend an estimated 2–5 minutes of every peak hour signing in. Biometrics are not the easy fix in New York. Labor Law §201-a bars employers from requiring fingerprints ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/201-A)); a 2011 state opinion letter applied that rule to fingerprint time clocks even when no image is stored, while leaving voluntary use allowed ([Halpern Advisors](https://www.halpernadvisors.com/biometric-devices-in-time-clocks-may-violate-n-y-labor-law/)). Aloha's own documentation warns that poor enrollment, grease or skin damage produce "Unable to Recognize Fingerprint" ([Aloha](https://delightful-coast-06c41e60f.3.azurestaticapps.net/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/fingerprint_scanner/implementing_fingerprint_scanner)).

### Reliability and close-out decide the rush, not taps per drink

In the reviews sampled, the most common complaint about every system is failure in the middle of a shift: freezes, crashes, lost tickets and tabs, offline modes that do not work, and updates that break things. TouchBistro users reported crashes "Like 6+ times per shift" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140677/TouchBistro/reviews/)). A SpotOn GM said a tab "just completely disappears sometimes!" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/197473/SpotOn-Restaurant/reviews/)), and a Toast chef manager wrote, "We always dread seeing an update" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=2)). Square's September 2023 outage lasted about a day and cost one pizzeria at least $651 in sales and $90 in tips ([Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/square-outage-disrupts-business-restaurants-nationwide)). Printers that fail to print or print tickets twice appear across most of the systems sampled ([Capterra Square](https://www.capterra.com/p/175628/Square-Point-of-Sale/reviews/); [Capterra TouchBistro](https://www.capterra.com/p/140677/TouchBistro/reviews/)). The sample leans toward owners: one page of 28 Toast reviewers included no server or bartender ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=4)), and Reddit's bartender forums could not be reached, so frontline voices are underrepresented.

Where speed has actually been measured, the gains came from removing waits and walks rather than taps. Guests ordering on tabletop devices cut dining time by 17%, and ordering and paying on them cut it by **31%** ([Susskind & Curry 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1938965518797080)); across 66 restaurants and 2.6 million checks, meal duration fell 9.74% and sales per minute rose 10.77% ([Tan & Netessine](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/2017.9.10_Tabletop_For_Submission_WithNames.pdf)). The known bar bottleneck is close-out. A payments vendor's CEO claimed in 2017 that the average bar tab takes 15 minutes to settle and described bartenders serving nothing for half an hour at close ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/exclusive-series/qsr-and-mobile/2017/rooam-streamlines-settling-up-junaid-shams-mobile-pay/)). Another vendor describes last call as the moment when "dozens of guests may want to pay at once," and tips push totals past their holds ([Bonsai](https://www.bonsaipos.com/bar-tabs-and-card-pre-authorisation-holds-walkouts-and-closing-out-at-last-call/)). Accuracy matters as much as speed: in 2025 drive-thru testing, orders were right 87% of the time, and customizations caused most of the errors ([Intouch Insight](https://www.intouchinsight.com/press-releases/drive-thru-study-2025-press-release)). No credible published figure exists for drinks per bartender-hour, taps per order, or seconds to open or close a tab on any system.

### New York law fixes which frictions must survive

Some friction is the law. ABC Law §65 bars sales to anyone "actually or apparently" under 21 or "visibly intoxicated," and it gives sellers a defense if they relied on a government photo ID or hold an Alcohol Training Awareness Program certificate ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABC/65)). A successful ID scan adds a defense in liquor-authority proceedings, but only **four fields** may be kept: name, date of birth, ID number and expiration date ([ABCL §65-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABC/65-B)). The State Liquor Authority accepts New York Mobile ID only through DMV-keyed verification software, and a screenshot never counts ([SLA](https://sla.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/10/advisory_2024-2_-_use_of_new_york_state_mobile_id_for_the_sale_of_alcoholic_beverages_0.pdf)). Sales stop from **4 to 8 a.m.**, or until 10 a.m. on Sunday, and drinking must end by 4:30 a.m. ([ABCL §106](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABC/106)). The Dram Shop Act lets anyone injured by an intoxicated guest sue whoever unlawfully served that guest ([GOL §11-101](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/11-101)).

Tips carry their own rules. Managers are excluded from tip pools, pool records are mandatory, any charge presented as a gratuity must go to service staff in full, and only the pro-rated card fee on a tip may be deducted ([12 NYCRR 146-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/title-12/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-146/subpart-146-2)). The IRS treats an automatic gratuity as a service charge, not a tip ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8027)), so West 4's automatic 20% ([West 4](https://www.west4karaoke.com/)) and the tips guests add must stay separate on screen and in payroll. The city's cashless ban means cash must work wherever guests pay on the premises ([DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/businesses/CashlessBanGuidance-AppBasedTransactions.pdf)). Tip screens have measurable side effects. In New York taxis, higher suggested tips raised tips by more than 10% but increased no-tip rides by 1.7–2.8 percentage points ([Haggag & Paci](http://www.kareemhaggag.com/f/Default_Tips.pdf)), and 40% of American adults oppose suggested tip amounts on screens, even though 70% always or often tip at bars ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/2023/11/09/tipping-culture-in-america-public-sees-a-changed-landscape/)).

### Fourteen rooms add a second queue driven by the clock

The karaoke rooms change what a tab is. A room is a long-lived group tab whose bill grows with the clock rather than the order pad: at $10 per person per hour, a party of six accrues **$60 of room time every hour** before anyone orders a drink ([West 4](https://www.west4karaoke.com/)). A hold sized at check-in goes stale within the hour, and a hard cap like Lightspeed O-Series' would either stop service or fail silently. Guests arrive late, leave early and want to pay separately, so one room may need several cards and partial payments. One Square user's complaint that "Partial payment and leave tab open" was "very difficult" describes the room case exactly ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/175628/Square-Point-of-Sale/reviews/)).

Orders that ring the bar from 14 rooms compete with the crowd at the rail. West 4's prototype requires the bar to accept each room order, which is the right checkpoint, but the bartender who accepts it never sees the guest who will drink it. Room staff can hear neither guests nor alerts, and room walls and soundproofing are likely to weaken Wi-Fi, although no source tested that. The front desk runs a separate job of check-ins, ID checks, extensions, moves and checkouts, and last call coincides with the 4 a.m. stop and the 4:30 a.m. clear-out. Surprise bills are the complaint rivals hear most. Maru reviewers describe $60-an-hour rooms ending in bills over $500 ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g60763-d4727661-Reviews-Maru_Karaoke_Lounge-New_York_City_New_York.html)), and a Boho Orchard guest objected to $47 of gratuity on a $200 personal share ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g60763-d5835028-Reviews-Karaoke_Boho_Orchard-New_York_City_New_York.html)). A per-minute bill that staff cannot explain at 3 a.m. becomes a dispute at the door.

## The West 4 design turns every control into a visible state

The design rests on one idea: the tab, whether a bar tab or a room, is the unit of work, and everything a person needs to act on it appears on its face. Three rules follow. Each task takes one visit, so nobody returns to a tab to type a tip, find a manager or check an ID again. Controls become states on the tab rather than dialogs in the way. And nothing a bartender touches waits on the internet. The design keeps what West 4's prototype already does well, namely the live room board, bar acceptance of room orders, the PIN-stamped action log and the QR waitlist, and reworks the parts that peak service will break. Every speed target below is a proposal to validate by timing real staff in the venue.

### Each role lands on its own screen, and identity is a tap

West 4 should replace mutually exclusive modes, Square's model, with role home screens on any device, following Aloha's default screen per job ([Aloha](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/field_definitions/jobcodes)) rather than Toast's per-device Primary Mode. A bartender signs in to the rail: open tabs down one side, the drink grid in the middle, the current check on the other side, and a strip of pending room orders across the top. The front desk opens on the 14-room board with the waitlist beside it. A room server's handheld opens on "my rooms," the rooms in that server's section, with one-tap switching between them, which answers the Toast reviewer who asked for a "next seat" button. A manager's phone or handheld opens on an exceptions inbox, and the door opens on ID scanning. Everyone works on the same tab and room objects, so a hand-off needs no translation. The prototype's three roles of Owner, Manager and Staff become finer job roles for screens and permissions, while tip-pool eligibility stays a separate per-person setting.

Identity should come from something staff carry. An NFC badge or wristband tap signs a person in on any terminal or handheld, following Toast's Access Card, Aloha's RFID and Dallas Key readers and Lightspeed's QR codes, and the same credential must work on every device, which a 2026 reviewer says Toast's Go 3 swipe card does not ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/toast/reviews)). Handhelds stay signed in all shift behind a screen lock. On a shared rail terminal, a badge tap claims the screen and the session releases when the check closes, as Aloha's "Automatically exit on close check" does ([Aloha](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/field_definitions/jobcodes)). Idle timeouts lock the screen but never discard a half-built order, so an interrupted bartender resumes exactly where they left off. A short PIN is requested only for sensitive actions and as the fallback for a lost badge, and fingerprints are offered only as an opt-in alternative. The PIN-stamped log survives unchanged; only the typing disappears. Badge sign-in time has never been measured, but a tap without a secret sits closer to the 2.6-second slide than the 4.9-second PIN in the smartphone study, so it should recover much of the estimated 2–5 minutes an hour that retyped PINs cost.

### The order grid stays still, large and forgiving

The grid should never move by itself. The 20 to 40 drinks that make up most of the night sit in fixed positions in the center and lower center of the screen, where Fitts's law favors them and where screen edges offer no advantage on touchscreens ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/fitts-law/)). New items drop into reserved slots, and seasonal changes arrive as scheduled layout versions, never mid-shift. Each bartender may pin a personal favorites row, because staff may customize the grid but the system never reorders it. Primary buttons should measure **15 to 20 mm**, secondary buttons at least 12 mm, and nothing tappable should be under 10 mm. Handheld targets should be at least 14 mm, and buttons with opposite effects, such as Send and Void, at least 5 mm apart. These sizes combine the walking, wet-screen and corner evidence rather than coming from any single study. Every action must work with a single tap, because water turns gestures into false multi-touches, and a one-tap wipe lock that freezes input for about 10 seconds lets staff clean the glass. On the rail terminal, Send and Pay belong above the drip line; on handhelds, they belong within thumb reach but out of the corners.

Drinks are entered the way bartenders say them. Modifiers sit inline beside the item, as in Toast's Open View, with defaults preselected so that "Tito's" already means rocks. Mixers, garnishes, and "No," "Extra" and "Side" are one-tap chips that, unlike Square's conversational modifiers, combine with quantities. Well, call and premium pours are price-changing choices that are never skipped, the opposite of TouchBistro's Fast Bar, because a wrong pour on a room bill turns into a checkout dispute. A **Repeat Round** button, and "Same again" on each room tile, re-orders the previous round under Simphony's rules: only flagged items repeat, and if anything has sold out the system names that item instead of silently dropping it ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/F32325_01/doc.192/f32328/c_menu_items_repeat_rounds.htm)). Before anything is sent, the tab or room name sits at the top in the largest type on the screen above a read-back of items and modifiers, because customizations are where orders go wrong. Unsent changes need no confirmation; an Undo stays available for several seconds instead. Sold-out items stay visible with their price and an "86'd" label, which fixes a Toast complaint that out-of-stock items "do not show price when recalled" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/)). A void asks whether the drink was made, so that only unmade items return to stock, which answers another Toast reviewer's "vicious cycle" of voids restocking inventory ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/toast/reviews)). Taps show feedback within 20 ms and results within 100 ms, which requires item entry to run on the device rather than over the network.

### Tabs open card-first, name themselves and keep their guarantee

Starting a tab takes one gesture. The guest dips, taps or swipes at any point, including after the first round is rung, which Toast itself recommends so that the first hold covers the order ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Preauthorized-Cards-Decline-When-Closing)). A dip supplies the name. A tap brings up one-tap labels, such as the rail seat, "Room 7" or a short description, with the keyboard opening by itself for a first name, and the card brand and last four digits are always appended. Room tabs are named by room number, so the tap-to-pay naming gap disappears for every room guest. Staff find a tab by tapping the card again, by its last four digits, or by name, seat, room or description, or through a "my tabs" filter. Following Lightspeed O-Series, one card can carry only one open tab, and a second tap asks, "This card already has Tab 12. Add to it?"

The hold follows the bill. It starts at the larger of a floor or the first order, the pattern Square and SpotOn use, and it grows by incremental authorization as drinks and room minutes accrue, as GoTab's does ([GoTab](https://gotab.com/business-type/bar-pos)). Whether Stripe, West 4's processor, supports incremental holds on its card readers was not researched, and it must be confirmed before this design is built. If West 4 keeps any card surcharge, every increase must recalculate it, because Stripe lets a surcharge be lowered at capture but never raised ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge)). Every tab row shows its headroom in words and an icon, not just Lightspeed's green or yellow badge: "Hold $120 · $18 left." When headroom falls below a margin (Clover's documented 20% capture limit is a useful yardstick), the tab asks for re-authorization or a second card instead of blocking service. Rooms accept several cards, which Toast's one-card-per-check rule forbids, and moving or merging tabs and rooms carries every hold along, where Toast's combine drops them. Tabs are never deleted. Each keeps a full history, and a voided or closed tab can be recovered, because a tab that "disappears" costs more staff trust than any other failure.

### Fourteen tiles run the rooms and show the math

All 14 rooms fit on one front-desk screen, placed where they sit on the floor, with no scrolling; on a 15-inch terminal each tile can be roughly 8 cm wide. The room number is the largest text on the tile. The state shows as color, icon and word together: vacant, booked, checked in, singing, 10 minutes left, overtime, checking out, cleaning or out of service. Each tile also shows headcount, elapsed and remaining time, the running total, hold headroom, and badges for pending orders, calls, approvals, "ID ✓" and cut-offs. At 10 minutes the tile turns amber and reads "10 min," the room server's handheld vibrates, and the room's own screen shows the warning, as can the TV if Playbox, for which the research found no public management API, agrees to expose that control ([Playbox Remote](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/playbox-remote/id6755547370)). An on-screen warning spares staff from walking in, a recurring complaint about Maru ([Four Dollar Jacks](https://www.fourdollarjacks.com/bars-near-me/maru-karaoke-lounge)). At zero the tile flashes, the convention Anest uses for its room lights. Extensions take one tap, adding 15, 30 or 60 minutes from the tile or from the guest's room screen, and the system blocks them with a stated reason when the next booking needs the room or 4 a.m. approaches. That rule replaces the prototype's "let them stay" texts with a check the board enforces.

Because no market offers a precedent for per-minute billing, the math must always be visible: "4 guests × $10/h × 97 min = $64.67," with the rounding rule, the next rate change and any headcount changes shown on the tile, on the room screen and on the receipt. Comping minutes, the noraebang "service time," takes one tap and a reason, and it needs a manager above a set threshold. Pausing the clock for broken equipment needs a manager. Moving a party to another room and merging two rooms, first-class actions in Chinese systems, carry the clock, the tab and every hold intact. Check-in at the front desk scans the party's IDs, records the headcount against the three-guest minimum, attaches cards or applies the deposit, assigns the room and starts the clock. Checkout can happen at the front desk, at the bar or on a server's handheld: a draft check, a split, payment on the guest's screen, and then the tile flips to cleaning until someone signs off the checklist. On a server's handheld the same tiles appear for that server's rooms only, sized for walking.

### Room orders arrive as a queue, not an alarm

An alarm cannot be heard reliably over 81 dBA, so a room order should arrive as a card in a persistent queue across the top of the rail screen and on the bar display, showing the room number, items, age, "ID ✓" and cut-off state. Ticket headers change color as orders age, as on Toast's kitchen screens ([Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformKDSOverview.html)), and sound becomes optional. Alcohol still needs a person to accept it, but acceptance takes one tap on a card that already shows everything the bartender needs to decide. Soft drinks and food, which the prototype also holds for acceptance, should route straight to their stations, as Anest's category printers do. A runner claims the order on a handheld and taps "delivered," and that same screen is where the runner refuses service to a guest who is visibly intoxicated. An order left unaccepted for a set number of minutes starts flashing and then escalates to the front desk and the manager, as Meniu-kun does with repeated calls. Service calls stay separate from orders, carry a reason chip and reach screens, handheld vibration and optional wearable receivers, because K米's own documentation shows that phone alerts alone are unreliable. A discreet "I need help" option goes only to a manager or security and never appears on the room's screen.

### Payment happens once, and last call closes in bulk

The guest should finish paying in one visit, on a screen that faces them. That can be a customer display at the rail, like the 8-inch guest screen on Clover's Station Duo ([Tech.co](https://tech.co/pos-system/clover-pos-pricing)) or Toast Flex ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/restaurant-pos/bar-pos)), or a handheld that flips to the guest, like SpotOn's ([SpotOn](https://www.spoton.com/blog/spoton-unveils-exclusive-restaurant-point-of-sale-hardware/)). Tips come before the tap, Square's default, so there is no slip to key in later. Walk-up drinks under a threshold get dollar suggestions and larger checks get moderate percentages such as 18, 20 and 22, with Custom and No tip given equal weight, a compromise between the taxi evidence that high defaults raise tips and Pew's evidence of resentment. Room tabs state "20% gratuity included" in words and treat any extra tip as optional and separately recorded; this judgment call keeps the service charge and the tip apart for tax purposes and heads off the surprise-bill complaint. If a surcharge remains, the guest sees card and cash totals before paying, the way Clover resellers describe dual pricing ([Nextdoor Payments](https://nextdoorpayments.com/clover/for-bars)). Cash gets quick-amount buttons like Toast's and a change-due figure as large as Aloha's grey box, and the drawer opens only on a cash tender or a logged no-sale.

Splits cover every pattern bars use: by guest or seat, by item, evenly, and by fraction for shared bottles, as Square offers ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6432-payments-with-square-for-restaurants)). Rooms add a split of room time by headcount or by each guest's own minutes, the multi-player split that pool-hall systems already provide ([BilliardPOS](https://billiardpos.net/en/blog/best-pos-systems-for-pool-halls-stop-losing-money/)). A guest who leaves early pays a share, and the rest of the room stays open with its holds intact, the way GoTab splits checks without releasing the card guarantee ([GoTab](https://gotab.com/latest/revolutionizing-nightlife-gotabs-pos-solutions-for-bars-and-nightclubs)). Every share shows its own gratuity and tax.

Last call gets its own screen. A close-out board lists every open tab and room with its age, headroom and tip status, and a countdown starts at the venue's internal last call; one vendor suggests reminders 15 to 30 minutes before close ([Bonsai](https://www.bonsaipos.com/bar-tabs-and-card-pre-authorisation-holds-walkouts-and-closing-out-at-last-call/)). Guests can close their own tabs from a QR code on the receipt or the room screen, as Square's Scan to Pay allows ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8421-new-order-and-pay-capabilities-with-square-for-restaurants)), which spreads the crush. After the 4:30 a.m. clear-out, a manager reviews and approves **one batch** that closes abandoned card tabs to their held cards for the amount actually ordered, adding only a gratuity disclosed when the tab opened; this replaces the prototype's unreviewed 4 a.m. auto-close. Any remaining paper tips go into one grid on one screen. A blocking checklist of open tabs, unentered tips, unaccepted orders and drawer counts must be cleared before the batch settles, which prevents the silent tip losses Clover and Square users describe. The business day ends after the clear-out, not at 4 a.m. as in Toast's default.

### Approvals, cash, ID and cut-offs move to an exceptions lane

Approvals should interrupt only when money leaves or an action cannot be undone, the rule Nielsen Norman Group gives for confirmation dialogs, which lose their force when overused ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)). Before an order is sent, removing an item is simply undo. After it is sent, voids and comps under a threshold need only a reason code. Above the threshold, a manager approves by badge tap at the terminal or from a push notification on their own device, and the requester keeps serving while the check shows "approval pending." Confirmations name the stakes, as in "Void 2× Margarita — $28?", and never ask "Are you sure?" No manager code is ever typed where others can watch it, which answers the Toast complaint about exposed manager passwords. Permissions are set per action, so harmless fixes such as changing a seat, renaming a tab or moving an item between one's own tabs need no one's approval. Room actions that change the bill, including comped minutes, clock pauses, headcount reductions and removal of the automatic gratuity, pass through the same thresholds. Every exception feeds a nightly report that shows the closing manager each person's pattern.

Cash drawers keep their full controls without slowing the rail. Each drawer has a named owner and a starting count. Where two bartenders share a well, the drawer can be shared instead, with every sale carrying the badge of whoever rang it, as Aloha's rapid-access login allows; the owner has to choose between that speed and single-person accountability. No-sale openings require a reason. Closing counts should be blind, so staff enter the cash they counted before they see the expected figure; blind counts are common practice, but the research found no study of their effect. Variances above a threshold, and all deposits, need a second signature, following Toast's cash guidance ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/restaurant-cash-management)). Cash tips are declared at clock-out, as Aloha can require ([Aloha](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/field_definitions/jobcodes)), and staff see their own night broken into card tips, cash tips and their share of the automatic gratuity, because the three are treated differently for tax.

ID checks and alcohol controls become badges rather than prompts. The door or front desk scans each guest once and keeps only the four permitted fields, and the tab or room then shows "ID ✓ by Ana, 11:42 PM," so nobody asks again. Toast's WE SCAN integration uses the same pattern, placing a green banner on the check after one scan, but it works only online ([Toast Support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Using-WE-SCAN-ID-Scanning-Integration)); West 4's version should work offline. Mobile IDs go through a DMV-keyed verifier. A cut-off flag on a guest or a room blocks alcohol on every device, including the room's own ordering screen, until a manager overrides it with a reason, and every refusal is logged. From 4:00 a.m., alcohol greys out on every screen with the reason stated, and no override exists, because the legal state is unambiguous.

### Reliability, language and training are staff features

A custom system earns trust only by staying up. A local hub at the bar should run room clocks, tabs, the room-order queue, printers and room screens over the local network, and every device should show a clear offline banner and queue its orders rather than lose them. Staff should still be able to close checks to cash, or to stored offline card payments under a cap, which Toast's offline mode does not allow, while no new card holds open offline, which matches Toast. Updates never run during service; they roll out in stages with one-step rollback. Printers get health indicators, de-duplicated ticket numbers and a reprint history. The team should rehearse an outage drill before opening night.

Language and training are part of the interface. Each person picks the interface language, starting with Spanish, Chinese and Korean, while positions and icons stay identical across languages, so a Korean-speaking bartender and a Spanish-speaking one can train each other by pointing. A sandbox mode on the real devices, a first-shift mode with inline hints for splits, voids and tip entry, and "first-shift competence" as an explicit usability test suit a crew that turns over by half each year. The baseline comes from timing staff on the prototype during real peaks as they open a tab, add a round, repeat a round, move items between tabs, split a check, close with a tip, extend a room, void with approval and count a drawer, with errors logged by type. The proposed targets, all still to be validated, are a repeated round in **two taps** or fewer, a two- or three-drink order in five, a tab opened in **three seconds** and a held tab closed through tip and receipt in **ten**, with novices reported separately from veterans.

Toast and Square also set a floor West 4 must reach before any of these advantages count. Toast's Open View, its large Saved CC button and its quick-cash suggestions are designs to match, not beat, and so are Square's smart tip thresholds, automatic progression through required modifiers, fractional splits and sign-on-device screens that report a decline at once ([Square blog](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/inside-square/mastering-bar-tabs)). Toast Go 3's cellular fallback is a hardware edge that a Wi-Fi-only handheld cannot match in rooms with poor coverage, and owners' reports that staff learn Toast in a day set the training bar.

The strongest case for buying instead of building is reliability at scale. Incumbents bring hardened payments, certified hardware and round-the-clock support, and even so the complaint record shows them failing mid-shift. A small team's custom system has less margin for error, so its advantages matter only after it has survived a rehearsed outage and a real Saturday night. Several of these seams are also already closed elsewhere: GoTab grows holds and splits checks without releasing them, SpotOn advertises incremental authorization and remote approvals, and Aloha has per-role screens and badge sign-in. West 4's durable edge is therefore not any single feature but putting all of them on the same object as a timed room, which no mainstream vendor models; one pool-hall software vendor says Square and Clover have "zero concept of table sessions" ([CuePoint](https://www.cuepoint.cloud/blog/best-pos-system-for-pool-halls)).

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the design question from how few taps a drink takes to how few second visits a tab needs. The measured gains in hospitality came from removing walks and waits, and they went mostly to the weakest staff, who in a crew that turns over by half each year are a large and constantly renewed share of the floor. Nearly every place where Toast and Square cost bartenders time at peak is a return trip to a tab that should already be finished: typing a name for a tapped card, re-running a declined hold, keying a slip tip, fetching a manager, re-entering a PIN. That suggests one headline metric for West 4's pilot, second visits per tab and per room, tracked alongside error types and reported separately for novices. The same logic resolves the apparent conflict between speed and control. A control expressed as a state on the tab (headroom, "ID ✓," cut-off, pending approval) costs nothing until it matters, and the rarer a confirmation, the more carefully it is read.

The karaoke rooms turn the POS inside out, because the clock rather than the order pad is the main input. That makes the room board, not the menu, the front desk's home screen, makes a hold that follows time a requirement rather than a luxury, and makes on-screen billing math a staff tool as much as a courtesy to guests. Some tensions remain open and should be settled in the venue rather than on paper: light versus dark themes under 14 lux, shared versus personal drawers at a two-person well, and how strongly to prompt for extra tips on rooms that already carry a 20% gratuity. The order of work follows from the risks. Confirm Stripe's support for incremental holds and Playbox's willingness to integrate, build the local hub and rehearse an outage, then time a Saturday on the prototype, because a staff-first POS that freezes at 1 a.m. is not staff-first.
